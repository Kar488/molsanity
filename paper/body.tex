\section{Introduction}

A medicinal chemist is shown a graph neural network's prediction that a
candidate is hERG-active, together with a heat map over its atoms. The heat map
concentrates on a piperidine nitrogen; the chemist finds that plausible, and the
series is deprioritised. Nothing in that workflow ever established that the
highlighted atoms are the atoms the model actually used, that they are the atoms
that matter chemically, or that the highlighting behaves the same way on the
next scaffold. Each of those is a separate question, and the answers come apart.

They come apart in a way that is measurable, and that gets worse in exactly the
setting drug discovery operates in. An attribution can be \emph{faithful} ---
the atoms it ranks highest really are the atoms whose removal moves the model's
output most --- and simultaneously \emph{wrong} --- those atoms are not the
substructure that determines the property. Across the \nGtCells{} committed
cells in this study that carry a node-level ground truth,
\nFaithfulButWrong{} are positively faithful to the model and yet
\emph{anti}-aligned with the true motif (ground-truth AUROC below the chance
value of $0.5$). A benchmark that scores only faithfulness rates all of them as
fine.

The sharpest form of the problem is a selection problem. If you have no ground
truth --- which is the situation on every real molecular dataset in this study
--- you must choose an attributor using a faithfulness or fidelity metric. On
the two cell families that do have a ground truth and were audited on both
splits, we can check whether that choice is the right one while changing
\emph{only} the split. Under a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split on MUTAG, all
$\mutShiftNMetrics{}$ ranking metrics --- our occlusion measure, Fidelity+ and
the GraphFramEx characterisation score alike --- select \mutShiftPickOccspearman{},
whose agreement with the mutagenic nitro motif is GT AUROC
$\mutShiftPickGtOccspearman{}$, while the ground truth ranks \mutShiftGtBest{}
best at $\mutShiftGtBestVal{}$. The median per-molecule gap is
$\mutShiftGapOccspearman{}$ AUROC (paired Wilcoxon
$p\mutShiftPOccspearman{}$, $n=\mutShiftNPairedOccspearman{}$), and the rank
correlation between faithfulness and correctness is \emph{negative}
($\mutShiftRhoOccspearman{}$ to $\mutShiftRhoCharacterization{}$). In
distribution on the same dataset, two of the three metrics instead select the
ground-truth-best attributor and the rank correlation is
$+\mutIndRhoOccspearman{}$.

This is not an argument that faithfulness metrics are wrong. It is an argument
that they answer a different question from the one a chemist is asking, and
that the gap between the two questions widens under distribution shift.
Molecular property prediction is deployed under scaffold shift almost by
construction --- the point of a screening model is to score series the training
set does not contain --- and the standard evaluation protocol for explanations
does not stratify by that shift at all.

\paragraph{Contributions} This paper presents \textbf{MolSanity}, a
reliability-audit framework for feature attributions on molecular GNNs. It is
not a new attribution method: it wraps the canonical implementations
(Captum~\cite{captum}, PyTorch Geometric~\cite{pyg}, RDKit~\cite{rdkit}) and
audits them. Specifically:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item We define a six-axis audit over a motif-native (RDKit) decomposition:
  coherence, occlusion--attribution faithfulness, ground-truth localisation,
  cross-checkpoint stability, calibration linkage and confidence/correctness
  regime stratification, with field-standard Fidelity$\pm$, sparsity, the
  GraphFramEx characterisation score~\cite{graphframex} and the PyG/DIG
  unfaithfulness metric~\cite{dig} computed on the \emph{same} molecules for
  direct comparability (\nameref{sec:framework}).
\item We show that a faithfulness-only ranking picks the wrong attributor under
  scaffold shift, in a design where only the split varies, so the effect is not
  confounded with a change of dataset (\nameref{sec:selection}).
\item We show reliability is a property of the cell, not of the attributor: the
  backbone ordering does not survive the change of split
  ($\rho=\bbRankRho{}$ over $\bbRankN{}$ backbones on the exact-ground-truth
  set), and neither does the attributor ordering
  ($\rho=\mutAttrRankRho{}$ on MUTAG, $\synAttrRankRho{}$ on
  \synDataset{}). Faithfulness itself, by contrast, does \emph{not} shift
  systematically --- across the $\nSplitPairs{}$ cells audited on both splits
  the median change is $\medSplitOccDelta{}$ --- so what shift breaks is the
  \emph{relationship} between faithfulness and correctness, not the level of
  faithfulness.
\item Using the \nRecords{} per-molecule records this run committed, we
  stratify reliability by confidence/correctness regime and test the
  calibration-linkage hypothesis directly --- finding it holds per cell
  (median $\rho=\medCalibRho{}$, $\nCalibSigPos{}$ cells significantly positive
  vs.\ $\nCalibSigNeg{}$ negative) but \emph{reverses} when cells are pooled
  ($\pooledCalibRho{}$), a Simpson's-paradox trap for anyone reporting a single
  aggregate number.
\item We report the negatives with the positives: cells whose ground truth is
  degenerate, a dataset that trains but yields no usable node labels, rows
  carried over from an earlier run (labelled, never merged into a this-run
  aggregate), and the \nNoGtCells{} cells where the correctness axis simply
  does not exist and is marked as such rather than scored.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Attribution methods} The attributors audited here are the standard
ones. Gradient-family methods --- Saliency~\cite{saliency}, Input×Gradient,
Guided Backpropagation~\cite{guidedbp} and Integrated Gradients~\cite{ig} ---
are model-agnostic and cheap. Graph-specific methods learn a mask over the
input: GNNExplainer~\cite{gnnexplainer} optimises a soft edge/feature mask per
instance, PGExplainer~\cite{pgexplainer} amortises that into a parametric mask
network, and SubgraphX~\cite{subgraphx} searches connected subgraphs with
Monte-Carlo tree search under a Shapley objective. Graph analogues of
CAM/Grad-CAM were introduced by Pope et al.~\cite{gradcamgraph}; Yuan et
al.~\cite{gnnsurvey} survey the space. We wrap, never reimplement.

\paragraph{Evaluating explanations} Two evaluation traditions exist.
\emph{Faithfulness} measures whether the explanation tracks the model:
Fidelity$\pm$ and sparsity~\cite{gnnsurvey}, the GraphFramEx characterisation
score that combines them~\cite{graphframex}, and the unfaithfulness metric
shipped with DIG/PyG~\cite{dig}. \emph{Correctness} measures whether the
explanation recovers a known rationale, which requires ground-truth
substructures --- typically synthetic~\cite{graphxai}. The tension between the
two is known: Faber et al.~\cite{faber} argue that comparing against ground
truth is misleading when the trained model does not use that rationale, and
Sanchez-Lengeling et al.~\cite{sanchezlengeling} built the first systematic
attribution benchmark for molecular GNNs on exactly this basis. Outside graphs,
sanity checks~\cite{sanitychecks} and retraining-based benchmarks~\cite{roar}
made a parallel case that plausible-looking attributions can be uninformative.
MolSanity's position is that neither axis subsumes the other, and that the
\emph{joint} behaviour of the two under distribution shift is the object of
interest.

\paragraph{Frameworks, and the gap} GraphXAI~\cite{graphxai} supplies synthetic
generators with exact ground truth and an evaluation library;
GraphFramEx~\cite{graphframex} systematises fidelity-style evaluation across
explainers and tasks; DIG~\cite{dig} packages methods and metrics; MolFaith
\cite{molfaith} evaluates feature attributions on molecular property models
under fidelity-based metrics. Table~\ref{tab:related} places MolSanity against
them. The claim is deliberately narrow: no individual row of that table is new.
What is new is the combination --- ground-truth localisation \emph{and}
faithfulness \emph{and} cross-checkpoint stability \emph{and} calibration
linkage, computed over a motif-native decomposition, stratified by scaffold
shift, across five backbones, with the field-standard metrics recomputed on the
same molecules so the comparison is head-to-head rather than cross-paper.

\section{The MolSanity audit}
\label{sec:framework}

\paragraph{Notation} A molecule is a graph $G=(V,E)$ with node features
$X\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|\times d}$ and edge features. A model $f$ maps $G$ to a
logit vector (classification) or a scalar (regression); $t$ denotes the
explained target. An attributor $A$ produces node attributions
$a=A(f,G,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|}$. RDKit decomposes $G$ into motifs
$\mathcal{M}=\{M_1,\dots,M_K\}$, $M_k\subseteq V$, from SSSR rings, the
Bemis--Murcko scaffold~\cite{murcko} and fragment decomposition; the motif score
is $s_k=\sum_{v\in M_k}\max(a_v,0)$. Working at motif rather than atom
granularity is what makes the audit chemically legible: a chemist reasons about
a nitro group, not about atom 14.

\paragraph{Coherence} Does the attribution concentrate mass sensibly? We report
the Gini coefficient of $a$, the mass in the top 20\% of atoms, the largest
connected-component fraction among salient atoms, and the motif top-1 share
$\max_k s_k/\sum_k s_k$. Coherence is necessary but never sufficient: an
attribution can be perfectly concentrated on the wrong motif.

\paragraph{Occlusion faithfulness} For each motif we zero its atoms' features
through the model's node-mask multiplier and measure the drop in the target
logit, $\Delta_k=f_t(G)-f_t(G\setminus M_k)$. Faithfulness is
$\rho=\mathrm{Spearman}(s,\Delta)$ over the motifs of a molecule, plus the
top-1 agreement indicator $\mathbb{1}[\arg\max_k s_k=\arg\max_k \Delta_k]$.
\textbf{Higher $\rho$ means more faithful}: the attributor ranks motifs the way
occlusion does. $\rho<0$ means the attributor ranks motifs in the reverse order
of their causal effect on the model --- worse than an uninformative
explanation. For regression, $\Delta$ is taken in output space rather than
probability space. We also compute Fidelity+ (target-probability drop when the
salient atoms are removed; higher is better), Fidelity$-$ (drop when the
non-salient atoms are removed; lower is better), sparsity, the GraphFramEx
characterisation score (their weighted harmonic mean~\cite{graphframex}) and
the PyG/DIG unfaithfulness metric~\cite{dig}, on the same molecules and the
same masks.

\paragraph{Ground-truth localisation} Where a node-level ground-truth mask
$g\in\{0,1\}^{|V|}$ exists, correctness is the AUROC/AUPRC of the normalised
$a$ against $g$. Chance is $0.5$; below $0.5$ the attribution is anti-aligned.
When $g$ is degenerate (all-0 or all-1) the metric is undefined and reported as
such --- never as $0$. Only the synthetic sets carry exact $g$; MUTAG carries a
chemically motivated nitro/aromatic-nitro SMARTS proxy after Debnath et
al.~\cite{mutag}, and we label it a proxy everywhere. No molecular dataset in
the sweep ships per-atom explanation labels, which is precisely why the
synthetic anchor is needed.

\paragraph{Cross-checkpoint stability} An attribution that changes when the
model is retrained a little is a weak basis for a chemistry decision. We keep an
early checkpoint of the same run and report
$\mathrm{Spearman}(s^{\text{early}},s^{\text{final}})$ per molecule. This axis
is absent from the frameworks in Table~\ref{tab:related}.

\paragraph{Calibration linkage and regime stratification} Predictions are
temperature-scaled on validation~\cite{calibration} and we report test expected
calibration error (ECE) alongside the attribution metrics. Per molecule, the
audit assigns a regime from calibrated confidence and correctness ---
\emph{confident-correct}, \emph{confident-error} (the dangerous one) and
\emph{borderline}, at a confidence threshold of $0.8$ --- and stratifies the
reliability metrics by it. Calibration linkage is the per-cell Spearman
correlation between $1-|{\rm confidence}-{\rm correct}|$ and a reliability
metric: positive would mean better-calibrated predictions carry more
trustworthy attributions.

\paragraph{Aggregation} Cells are compared with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests on shared molecules and bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over
molecules. $p$-values are reported unadjusted and treated as descriptive.

\paragraph{Engineering} The pipeline is staged and idempotent: each stage writes
a \texttt{.done} marker keyed to a content hash of its config, every cell is
wrapped so a failure is logged and the run continues, and trained checkpoints
are committed so the audit reproduces without retraining. Seeds, library
versions, git revision and hardware are recorded in a per-run manifest.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets} Tier-1 provides the ground-truth anchor:
\textbf{SynthMotifs} and its larger twin \textbf{SynthMotifsXL}, generated
offline (Barab\'asi--Albert base plus house/cycle motifs) with \emph{exact}
node ground truth, and \textbf{MUTAG}~\cite{mutag,tudataset} with the nitro
proxy. Tier-2 is MoleculeNet~\cite{moleculenet}: \textbf{BBBP} and \textbf{BACE}
(classification), \textbf{ESOL}, \textbf{FreeSolv} and \textbf{Lipophilicity}
(regression). Tier-3 covers chemistry-meets-medicine endpoints:
\textbf{ClinTox}, \textbf{SIDER} and \textbf{Tox21} as single-task binary views
of the MoleculeNet panels, and \textbf{DILI} (drug-induced liver injury) and
\textbf{hERG} (cardiotoxicity) via the Therapeutics Data Commons~\cite{tdc}.
TDC sets are featurised with the same PyG encoding as MoleculeNet so the
backbones are drop-in. BA-2Motifs and ShapeGGen~\cite{graphxai} are attempted
and gracefully skipped: the former's download returns HTTP 403 in this
environment, the latter's install depends on the pre-2.5 PyG compiled
extensions. Credential-gated resources are out of scope and never worked
around.

\paragraph{Backbones} GINE~\cite{gin,pretraingnn}, GCN~\cite{gcn},
GAT~\cite{gat}, MPNN~\cite{mpnn} and AttentiveFP~\cite{attentivefp}, sharing a
pooling head, calibration and splits.

\paragraph{Attributors} Integrated Gradients, Saliency, Guided Backpropagation
and Input×Gradient through Captum~\cite{captum}; GNNExplainer and PGExplainer
through PyTorch Geometric~\cite{pyg}. SubgraphX~\cite{subgraphx} requires DIG,
which installs but cannot import in this environment (no
\texttt{torch\_\-sparse} wheel at the pinned torch version), so it is left
blocked rather than approximated. PGExplainer is classification-only by
construction: its mask network trains against class logits, so it is absent
from the regression cells.

\paragraph{Splits} The primary split is a deterministic, class-aware
Bemis--Murcko scaffold split~\cite{murcko} (whole scaffold groups move together,
so there is no leakage, but every fold is guaranteed to contain every class),
with a random split as the in-distribution reference. Imbalanced classification
uses inverse-frequency class weighting and AUC-based model selection. Every
comparison in this paper that is labelled ``shift'' versus ``in-distribution''
is these two splits on the same dataset.

\paragraph{The run} All results are from the committed \texttt{\runConfig{}}
sweep, run \runStamp{} on a single CUDA device with seed \runSeed{}
(torch~\runTorch{}, PyG~\runPyG{}, RDKit~\runRDKit{}, Captum~\runCaptum{};
manifest in \texttt{results/artifacts/run\_manifest.json}). It attempted
\nAttempted{} cell-runs and committed \nRecords{} per-molecule audit records
across \nRecordCells{} cells.

Table~\ref{tab:tier1} lists every committed cell in which attribution
\emph{correctness} can be measured at all, and Figure~\ref{fig:faithcorrect}
plots the joint distribution. Of the \nGtCells{} cells with both metrics
defined, \nAntiAligned{} are anti-aligned with the ground truth (GT AUROC
$<0.5$) and \nFaithfulButWrong{} of those are simultaneously positively
faithful to the model. That combination is the failure mode a faithfulness-only
audit cannot see, because from the model's point of view nothing is wrong.

The clearest molecular instance is the MUTAG scaffold block of
Table~\ref{tab:tier1}, where dataset, backbone and split are fixed and only the
attributor varies. Guided Backpropagation, Saliency and Input×Gradient are the
three \emph{most} faithful attributors on that cell
($\rho=\mutShiftGBPOcc{}$, $\mutShiftSalOcc{}$, $\mutShiftIxGOcc{}$) and the
three \emph{worst} at recovering the nitro motif (GT AUROC
$\mutShiftGBPGt{}$, $\mutShiftSalGt{}$, $\mutShiftIxGGt{}$). GNNExplainer,
which the ground truth ranks best at $\mutShiftGNNEGt{}$, is less faithful
($\mutShiftGNNEOcc{}$) than all three. On this cell the two axes are not merely
uncorrelated; they are inverted.

\subsection{A fidelity-only ranking picks the wrong attributor under shift}
\label{sec:selection}

\input{generated/tab_selection}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_dissociation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Under scaffold shift a faithfulness-only ranking selects the
wrong attributor; in distribution it mostly does not.} Ground-truth
localisation per attributor, with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over
molecules. Within each row the dataset, backbone and attributor set are fixed
and only the split changes. \textbf{(a)} MUTAG (motif-proxy ground truth):
in distribution two of three metrics select the ground-truth-best attributor;
under shift all three select \mutShiftPickOccspearman{}, the worst-localising
of the six, significantly so (marked~*). \textbf{(b)} \synDataset{} (exact
node ground truth): the same direction with a smaller penalty --- in
distribution the mismatched picks are statistically indistinguishable from the
best (marked~$\dagger$), under shift they are not. Rank correlations between
each faithfulness metric and ground truth are given below each panel.}
\label{fig:dissociation}
\end{figure*}

If faithfulness and correctness dissociate, then ranking attributors by a
faithfulness metric --- what an evaluation framework must do when no ground
truth is available --- can select the wrong method. Two cell families let us
test this cleanly: each has a node-level ground truth, all
$\mutShiftNAttr{}$ attributors audited on the same molecules, and both splits
from this run, so the in-distribution and shift arms differ \emph{only} in the
split (Table~\ref{tab:selection}, Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}).

\paragraph{MUTAG, under shift} The ground truth ranks \mutShiftGtBest{} best
($\mutShiftGtBestVal{}$). All $\mutShiftNMetrics{}$ metrics instead rank
\mutShiftPickOccspearman{} first, at $\mutShiftPickGtOccspearman{}$ --- the
worst of the $\mutShiftNAttr{}$. On paired per-molecule GT AUROC over the
$\mutShiftNPairedOccspearman{}$ shared molecules the median gap is
$\mutShiftGapOccspearman{}$ with $p\mutShiftPOccspearman{}$. The
faithfulness--truth rank correlation is negative for every metric:
$\mutShiftRhoOccspearman{}$ for occlusion $\rho$,
$\mutShiftRhoFidelityplus{}$ for Fidelity+ and
$\mutShiftRhoCharacterization{}$ for characterisation. A framework ranking on
faithfulness here does not merely fail to find the best attributor; it
systematically prefers the worst.

\paragraph{MUTAG, in distribution} The same experiment on the random split
behaves differently. Occlusion $\rho$ and Fidelity+ both select
\mutIndPickOccspearman{}, which is also the ground-truth-best, and their rank
correlation with truth is $+\mutIndRhoOccspearman{}$. The characterisation
score is the exception, selecting \mutIndPickCharacterization{} at
$\mutIndPickGtCharacterization{}$ ($p\mutIndPCharacterization{}$) with rank
correlation $\mutIndRhoCharacterization{}$. So the sign flip between the two
arms is large --- $+\mutIndRhoOccspearman{}$ to
$\mutShiftRhoOccspearman{}$ for occlusion $\rho$ --- but it is not that
in-distribution evaluation is safe, only that it is much less dangerous.

\paragraph{The exact-ground-truth arm agrees, more mildly} On \synDataset{},
where node labels are exact rather than a motif proxy, the same pattern appears
with a smaller penalty. In distribution, occlusion $\rho$ picks the
ground-truth-best and the two field-standard metrics pick a neighbour whose GT
AUROC is statistically indistinguishable from it
($\synIndPickGtFidelityplus{}$ vs $\synIndPickGtOccspearman{}$,
$p=\synIndPFidelityplus{}$). Under shift all three select
\synShiftPickOccspearman{} ($\synShiftPickGtOccspearman{}$) over the
ground-truth-best \synShiftGtBest{} ($\synShiftGtBestVal{}$), significantly so
($p\synShiftPOccspearman{}$), though here the rank correlations stay positive
($\synShiftRhoOccspearman{}$ to $\synShiftRhoCharacterization{}$). The
direction replicates across an exact and a proxy ground truth; the severity
does not.

\paragraph{Two caveats we do not paper over} First, our own faithfulness
metric fails alongside the field-standard ones on the MUTAG shift arm. The
conclusion is not that MolSanity's occlusion measure is a better selector; it
is that under shift \emph{no} faithfulness metric in this experiment carries
reliable information about which attributor is correct --- an argument for
measuring correctness, not for a better proxy. Second, $\nDegenerateGtCells{}$
of the ground-truth cells are degenerate in a way that matters here: every
audited molecule returns an identical GT AUROC of $\degenVal{}$, including
\degenCell{} ($n=\degenN{}$). That cell is the one the in-distribution MUTAG
arm names as ground-truth-best, so the ``no mismatch'' verdict there rests on a
cell with zero between-molecule variance and should not be read as a positive
result. The shift arm, which carries the argument, does not involve it.

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} lays the two axes over the audited grid, and two
further comparisons show that neither axis is a stable property of the
attributor or of the architecture.

\paragraph{Backbones} Holding the attributor fixed at Integrated Gradients and
varying the backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:backbone}) gives a spread of
$\bbIndSpread{}$ AUROC in distribution (\bbIndBest{} $\bbIndBestVal{}$ down to
\bbIndWorst{} $\bbIndWorstVal{}$) and $\bbShiftSpread{}$ under shift
(\bbShiftBest{} $\bbShiftBestVal{}$ down to \bbShiftWorst{}
$\bbShiftWorstVal{}$) --- comparable to the spread across attributors on the
same dataset. The two orderings are negatively correlated
($\rho=\bbRankRho{}$, $\bbRankN{}$ backbones): the architecture that localises
the motif best in distribution is not the one that does so under shift. The
attributor ordering is somewhat more robust across the same change of split
($\rho=\armAttrRankRho{}$ over $\armAttrRankN{}$ attributors) but far from
preserved.

\paragraph{The shift itself does not lower faithfulness} It would be tidy if
scaffold shift simply made attributions less faithful. It does not. Across the
$\nSplitPairs{}$ cells this run audited on both splits, occlusion faithfulness
falls under the scaffold split in $\nSplitOccDrop{}$ of them --- about half ---
with a median change of $\medSplitOccDelta{}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:distributions}b).
We reported the opposite trend in an earlier, partial version of this matrix;
with the grid complete it does not hold. What shift damages is the
\emph{relationship} between faithfulness and correctness, not the level of
faithfulness, which is precisely why a faithfulness metric gives no warning
that anything has changed.

Because this run committed its per-molecule records, two audit axes that
previous drafts could only define are now measurable
(Table~\ref{tab:regime}, Figure~\ref{fig:regime}).

\paragraph{Regime stratification} Pooling the \nRegimeMol{} audited molecules
by regime gives the audit's most directly actionable result. Ground-truth
localisation is highest on confident-correct molecules ($\regCCGt{}$,
$n=\nRegCCGt{}$), drops to $\regBLGt{}$ on borderline ones
($n=\nRegBLGt{}$), and falls to $\regCEGt{}$ --- \emph{below chance} --- on
\emph{confident-error} molecules ($n=\nRegCEGt{}$). When the model is
confidently wrong, its attribution does not merely fail to help: it points away
from the substructure that matters. That is the regime a deployment cares most
about and the one in which an explanation is most likely to be believed.
Occlusion faithfulness gives no warning: it is $\regCEOcc{}$ on those same
confident-error molecules, no worse than the $\regCCOcc{}$ of the
confident-correct ones. The confident-error ground-truth estimate rests on
$\nRegCEGt{}$ molecules and is reported with that $n$ throughout; it is
suggestive rather than precise.

\paragraph{Calibration linkage} The framework's hypothesis is that
better-calibrated predictions carry more trustworthy attributions. Computed per
cell it is supported on balance --- median $\rho=\medCalibRho{}$ across
$\nCalibCells{}$ classification cells, $\nCalibSigPos{}$ significantly positive
against $\nCalibSigNeg{}$ significantly negative --- but the effect is
cell-specific rather than universal. Pooling every molecule into a single
correlation instead yields $\pooledCalibRho{}$: the opposite sign. The pooled
number is an artefact of mixing cells with different confidence distributions,
and we report it only to warn against it.

\paragraph{Regression is mostly, but not uniformly, anti-faithful} Across the

are inverted. The honest statement is that our regression faithfulness operator
needs redefinition, and we flag it rather than build on it.

\paragraph{Coherence is high nearly everywhere, and says little} Motif top-1

these medians are descriptive rather than a controlled comparison.

\subsection{Negative and degenerate results}

We record what did not work, because it carries information.

\paragraph{Ground truth that cannot be scored} BA-2Motifs --- a synthetic
benchmark whose whole purpose is to carry exact node labels --- trains here and
produces $2\times100$ audited molecules, but our loader recovered no node-level
ground truth from it, so both its cells sit in the no-ground-truth block. It is
counted as audited, not as a ground-truth cell, and it is the clearest
outstanding gap in the matrix: recovering those labels would give a second
exact-ground-truth arm on which to replicate the selection experiment.

\paragraph{Degenerate ground truth} $\nDegenerateGtCells{}$ ground-truth cells
return the identical value $\degenVal{}$ on every audited molecule, including
\degenCell{} ($n=\degenN{}$). A cell with zero between-molecule variance
cannot support a confidence interval or a paired test, and one of the two is
the attributor the in-distribution MUTAG arm names as ground-truth-best. We
report those cells with the rest and mark what rests on them.

\paragraph{The best model has the worst attributions} The highest-AUC molecular
classification cell in the run is \bestAucDataset{}·\bestAucBackbone{} under the
scaffold split, at accuracy $\bestAucAcc{}$, ROC-AUC $\bestAucAuc{}$ and ECE
$\bestAucEce{}$ --- accurate and well calibrated. Its attributions are the most
\emph{anti}-faithful of any molecular cell in the matrix
($\rho=\bestAucOcc{}$): Integrated Gradients ranks the motifs of these
molecules in close to the reverse of their causal order. Predictive quality,
calibration and explanation reliability are three different axes.

\paragraph{A weak model can be faithfully explained} The converse also appears.
The most faithful molecular cell is
\mostFaithfulDataset{}·\mostFaithfulBackbone{} ($\rho=\mostFaithfulOcc{}$) on a
model with accuracy $\mostFaithfulAcc{}$ and ROC-AUC $\mostFaithfulAuc{}$ ---
barely better than chance. Faithfulness certifies that the explanation
describes the model; it says nothing about whether the model is worth
describing. Any reliability report that omits the predictive metrics can be
satisfied by this cell.

\paragraph{Accuracy is the wrong headline on imbalanced sets}
\nHighAccRows{} committed rows report test accuracy $\geq0.95$, with ROC-AUC as
low as $\minHighAccAuc{}$. On Tox21, accuracy runs $\toxAccMin{}$--$\toxAccMax{}$
against AUC $\toxAucMin{}$--$\toxAucMax{}$. We report AUC alongside accuracy
throughout for this reason, and the lowest-AUC cell in the run
(\worstAucDataset{}·\worstAucBackbone{}, AUC $\worstAucAuc{}$ at accuracy
$\worstAucAcc{}$) is exactly the case where accuracy alone would mislead.

\paragraph{Blocked, not silently dropped} SubgraphX (via DIG) and ShapeGGen (via
GraphXAI) could not be installed: both need the pre-2.5 PyG
\texttt{torch\_\-sparse}/\texttt{torch\_\-scatter} compiled extensions, for
which no wheel exists at the pinned torch version. BA-2Motifs' download returns
HTTP 403 here. All are logged as blocked, occupy no row in any table, and are
excluded from every aggregate. ShapeGGen's exact-ground-truth role is covered by
the offline SynthMotifs generator; SubgraphX's absence is a genuine gap in
attributor coverage.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the audit buys} The single most useful thing MolSanity produces
is not a score but a \emph{negative certificate}: for \nNoGtCells{} of the
\nRowsTotal{} committed rows --- every real molecular dataset in the sweep ---
attribution correctness cannot be measured at all, because no per-atom
explanation labels exist. Those cells can be audited for faithfulness,
coherence, stability and calibration, and this paper's central result is that
under shift none of those is a reliable stand-in for correctness. Making that
gap explicit is more useful than filling it with a metric that looks like
correctness and is not.

\paragraph{How attributions should be reported} Four practices follow directly
from the matrix. (i) Report the regime: an attributor selected in distribution
carries no warrant out of it, and on the one cell family where we can check,
the selection is significantly wrong under shift. (ii) Report the backbone: the
backbone ordering does not survive the change of split ($\rho=\bbRankRho{}$),
so ``IG on a GNN'' is under-specified. (iii) Report the predictive metrics next
to the explanation metrics, because the accurate, well-calibrated
\bestAucDataset{}·\bestAucBackbone{} cell has the most anti-faithful
attributions in the matrix. (iv) Do not pool: the calibration-linkage
correlation changes sign between the per-cell and the pooled analysis.

\paragraph{Why reliability is regime-, backbone- and dataset-dependent} A
faithfulness metric asks about the model; a ground-truth metric asks about the
chemistry; they agree only when the model has learned the chemistry. That
coincidence is engineered into synthetic benchmarks and is not guaranteed on
molecular data, particularly out of distribution --- the situation Faber et
al.~\cite{faber} identify as making ground-truth comparison hazardous in
general. Attribution evaluation inherits the model's inductive limitations,
which is why the correct object of study is the cell --- (dataset, backbone,
attributor, split) --- and not the attributor.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Ground truth is scarce, and one arm of it is a proxy} The two cell
families that carry the argument have $n=\mutShiftNMol{}$ audited molecules per
arm. MUTAG, the molecular one, uses a chemically motivated nitro/aromatic-nitro
SMARTS proxy rather than annotator labels: it encodes an assumption about which
substructure ought to matter, and the trained model may use a different,
equally valid rationale~\cite{faber}. \synDataset{}, where the labels are
exact, is a structural non-molecular graph task. The inversion replicates
across both, which is the strongest available evidence, but neither arm is
simultaneously molecular and exactly labelled --- no public dataset is, which
is the gap this paper is about.

\paragraph{Degenerate ground-truth cells} $\nDegenerateGtCells{}$ cells return
an identical GT AUROC on every audited molecule, including \degenCell{}
($\degenVal{}$, $n=\degenN{}$). Zero between-molecule variance means the
bootstrap interval is uninformative and any ``best attributor'' verdict resting
on such a cell is fragile; we flag them rather than drop them, and the shift
arms that carry the argument do not involve them.

\paragraph{A dataset that trains but cannot be scored} BA-2Motifs loads and
trains in this run, but the loader recovered no node-level ground truth from
it, so its cells contribute faithfulness only and appear in the no-ground-truth
block despite being a synthetic benchmark designed to have exact labels.
Recovering those labels would add a second exact-ground-truth arm and is the
most valuable single fix outstanding.

\paragraph{Single split, single seed} Each cell is one deterministic split at
one seed. Cross-checkpoint stability partially probes run-to-run variability,
but no confidence interval in this paper covers split or seed variance, and the
per-cell molecule counts (20 on the synthetic arms) make individual contrasts
underpowered.

\paragraph{Occlusion as a counterfactual} Zeroing node features takes the graph
off the data manifold; a motif whose removal barely moves the output may be
redundant rather than unimportant. This bears most heavily on the regression
cells, where the output-space Fidelity$\pm$ values leave the range a
probability-space fidelity occupies and we decline to interpret the sign.

\paragraph{Proxy ground truth} MUTAG's mask is a nitro/aromatic-nitro SMARTS
proxy, not annotator labels. It encodes an assumption about which substructure
ought to matter, and the trained model may use a different, equally valid
rationale~\cite{faber}.

\paragraph{Coverage of attributors} SubgraphX is absent for dependency reasons
and PGExplainer failed in this run, so conclusions about the perturbation
family rest on GNNExplainer alone in the current-run cells.

\paragraph{Multiplicity} $p$-values are unadjusted across a large number of
paired comparisons; individual contrasts in Table~\ref{tab:paired} should be
read as descriptive.

\section{Conclusion}

Molecular GNN attributions are audited today for whether they describe the
model. Across the committed audit matrix we find that this is not the same
question as whether they describe the chemistry, and that the two diverge in
the regime molecular property prediction actually operates in. On the one cell
family where exact ground truth, several attributors and both splits coexist, a
faithfulness-only ranking is harmless in distribution and selects a
significantly worse attributor under scaffold shift --- and MolSanity's own
faithfulness metric is no exception. Reliability does not transfer across
backbones or across splits, and for the \nNoGtCells{} molecular rows in the
matrix, correctness cannot be measured at all with the labels the field
currently has. The audit is what makes those failures visible; the pipeline,
the figures and every number here regenerate from the committed artifacts as
the grid fills in.
