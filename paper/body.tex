\section{Introduction}

Graph neural networks (GNNs) are now standard for molecular property prediction
(MPP), and post-hoc feature attribution is the standard way practitioners ask
\emph{why} a model made a call. A medicinal chemist looking at a toxicity
prediction wants to know which substructure drove it. Integrated Gradients
\cite{sundararajan2017}, GNNExplainer \cite{ying2019} and PGExplainer
\cite{luo2020} all return such a map, and all of them return one whether or not
the map means anything.

The prevailing way to check that a map means something is \emph{faithfulness}:
does the explanation track the model's own computation? Fidelity$\pm$ and related
perturbation scores \cite{yuan2023survey}, and evaluation frameworks built on
them \cite{amara2022,agarwal2023,liu2021dig}, all operationalise this idea.
Faithfulness is necessary --- an explanation unmoored from the model is useless
--- but it is not sufficient. A model can key on a spurious correlate, and an
attribution can report that correlate perfectly. The explanation is then
maximally faithful and completely wrong about the chemistry. Sanity checks in
vision found exactly this class of failure \cite{adebayo2018}, and in molecules
the stakes are concrete: the attribution is read as a structure--activity claim.

Distribution shift sharpens the problem. MPP models are deployed on chemistry
unlike their training set, which is why Bemis--Murcko scaffold splits
\cite{bemis1996} are the accepted evaluation protocol
\cite{wu2018moleculenet,yang2019analyzing}. Attribution reliability under that
shift is, however, rarely measured at all.

\paragraph{A concrete failure.} Take MUTAG with a GINE backbone under a scaffold
split (Table~\ref{tab:headline}). Saliency attains occlusion faithfulness
$\rho=0.376$ and Fidelity$+$ $=0.267$ --- respectable by any faithfulness-first
standard, and the \emph{best} characterisation score of the six attributors we
test. Its ground-truth AUROC against the nitro-motif mask is $0.026$, where
$0.5$ is chance: it is strongly anti-aligned with the substructure the label is
built on. Integrated Gradients, at a very similar faithfulness ($\rho=0.414$,
Fidelity$+$ $=0.252$), reaches $0.540$. A practitioner ranking by the
field-standard scores would adopt the attributor that is confidently pointing at
the wrong atoms.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A reliability audit, not another attributor.} \textsc{MolSanity}
  composes occlusion faithfulness, ground-truth localisation, an atom- and
  motif-level coherence battery, cross-checkpoint stability and calibration
  linkage, and stratifies all of them by confidence/correctness regime under
  scaffold shift (\S\ref{sec:framework}). It wraps canonical implementations
  \cite{kokhlikyan2020,fey2019,landrum_rdkit} rather than reimplementing them.
\item \textbf{Evidence that faithfulness does not imply correctness.} Over
  \numNGtCells\ ground-truth-bearing cells the two are uncorrelated
  ($\rho=\numGlobalRho$, $p=\numGlobalP$); on MUTAG a \numGradOccSpread\ spread in
  faithfulness hides a \numGradGtSpread\ spread in correctness
  (\S\ref{sec:res-headline}).
\item \textbf{A regime-dependent selection failure, tested for significance.}
  With exact ground truth in-distribution, faithfulness-based ranking picks the
  correct attributor ($\rho=+0.94$, $p=0.005$); under scaffold shift Fidelity$+$
  and characterisation mis-select, with per-molecule paired Wilcoxon $p<0.001$
  (\S\ref{sec:res-regime}).
\item \textbf{Backbone- and task-dependence, with negatives kept in.}
  Localisation on exact ground truth orders GCN $>$ AttentiveFP $>$ MPNN $>$ GINE
  $>$ GAT, and the most accurate model is not the best explained
  (\S\ref{sec:res-backbone}); we report degenerate cells and a directionality
  caveat on the regression numbers rather than suppressing them
  (\S\ref{sec:res-negative}, \S\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Attribution methods.} Gradient-family attributions --- Saliency
\cite{simonyan2014}, Input$\times$Gradient \cite{shrikumar2016}, Guided
Backpropagation \cite{springenberg2015} and Integrated Gradients
\cite{sundararajan2017} --- transfer directly to graphs through Captum
\cite{kokhlikyan2020}. Graph-specific methods learn an explanatory mask:
GNNExplainer optimises one per instance \cite{ying2019}, PGExplainer amortises a
parametric mask generator across instances \cite{luo2020}, and SubgraphX searches
connected subgraphs with Shapley scoring \cite{yuan2021subgraphx}. We audit these
methods; we do not propose a new one.

\paragraph{Evaluating explanations.} Fidelity$\pm$ and sparsity are the
field-standard perturbation metrics \cite{yuan2023survey}. GraphFramEx
\cite{amara2022} systematises them and adds a characterisation score combining
Fidelity$+$ and Fidelity$-$; GraphXAI \cite{agarwal2023} supplies synthetic
graphs with ground-truth explanations and a metric suite; DIG \cite{liu2021dig}
packages methods and metrics as a library. In vision, \emph{Sanity Checks}
\cite{adebayo2018} showed some saliency maps are invariant to randomising the
model, and ROAR \cite{hooker2019} showed retraining-based evaluation can reverse
method rankings; faithfulness has also been argued to need careful definition
\cite{jacovi2020}. Our contribution is orthogonal to all of these: we measure
faithfulness \emph{and} ground-truth correctness on the same molecules and show
the two dissociate as a function of distribution shift.

\paragraph{Molecular attribution.} Sanchez-Lengeling et al.\ \cite{sanchez2020}
benchmark GNN attributions on synthetic molecular tasks with known ground truth,
and remains the closest prior work; McCloskey et al.\ \cite{mccloskey2019} use
attribution to recover binding mechanism, and Jim\'enez-Luna et al.\
\cite{jimenezluna2020} survey explainable AI for drug discovery. Relative to
these, \textsc{MolSanity} adds the shift axis (scaffold splits with regime
stratification), a motif-native audit built on RDKit ring/Murcko/BRICS
decompositions, stability across training checkpoints, calibration linkage, and
a head-to-head test of whether faithfulness-based model selection agrees with
ground truth.

\paragraph{Positioning.} GraphFramEx, GraphXAI and DIG answer ``is this
explanation faithful, and how does it score against a synthetic ground truth in
distribution?'' \textsc{MolSanity} answers ``\emph{when does faithfulness stop
predicting correctness}, and for which backbone, attributor and chemistry?''
Table~\ref{tab:selection} shows this is not a hypothetical distinction: the
GraphFramEx characterisation score selects the correct attributor
in-distribution and the wrong one under shift, on the same benchmark, with the
same code.

\section{The MolSanity audit}
\label{sec:framework}

\paragraph{Notation.} A molecule is a graph $G=(V,E)$ with $n=|V|$ atoms, node
features $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ and edge features. A trained model $f$ maps
$G$ to logits $f(G)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ (classification) or a scalar (regression).
An attributor $\mathcal{A}$ returns node scores
$a=\mathcal{A}(f,G)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\ge0}$; we use unsigned magnitudes
throughout and min--max normalise to $[0,1]$ where a common scale is needed.
RDKit gives a motif decomposition $\mathcal{M}=\{M_1,\dots,M_K\}$, $M_k\subseteq
V$, from smallest-set-of-smallest-rings, Bemis--Murcko scaffolds and BRICS
fragments. The motif-level score is $s_k=\sum_{i\in M_k} a_i$.

\paragraph{Occlusion faithfulness.} For each motif we mask its atoms and record
the drop in the explained-class logit,
\[
\Delta_k \;=\; f_c(G) - f_c\!\left(G \setminus M_k\right),
\]
and report $\rho_{\mathrm{occ}} = \mathrm{Spearman}(s_{1:K},\Delta_{1:K})$.
\textbf{Higher is more faithful}: the attribution ranks motifs the same way
occlusion does. $\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}<0$ means the attribution ranks motifs in the
\emph{opposite} order to their causal effect on the model. We also report
top-1 agreement, and the field-standard Fidelity$+$ (probability drop when the
salient atoms, top quintile, are removed; higher is better), Fidelity$-$
(the drop when non-salient atoms are removed; lower is better), sparsity, and the
GraphFramEx characterisation score, the weighted harmonic mean of Fidelity$+$
and $1-$Fidelity$-$ \cite{amara2022}.

\paragraph{Ground-truth correctness.} Where a per-atom ground-truth mask
$g\in\{0,1\}^n$ exists, correctness is the AUROC of the normalised attribution
against $g$, with AUPRC alongside. Chance is $0.5$; \emph{below} $0.5$ means
anti-alignment. AUROC is undefined for a degenerate mask and we emit \texttt{--}
rather than a number. Two sources of $g$ are used, and we keep them
distinct throughout: \textsc{SynthMotifs}/\textsc{SynthMotifsXL}, synthetic
Barab\'asi--Albert graphs with an injected house or 5-cycle motif, give
\emph{exact} node labels; MUTAG gives a chemically motivated nitro-group
\emph{proxy}, since it ships no annotator labels \cite{debnath1991}.

\paragraph{Coherence.} Independent of ground truth, an attribution should be
concentrated and chemically contiguous. We report the Gini coefficient of $a$,
the mass in the top $20\%$ of atoms, the fraction of salient atoms lying in a
single connected component, and the share of attribution mass in the top motif.

\paragraph{Cross-checkpoint stability.} Training saves an intermediate checkpoint
at one fifth of the epoch budget. Stability is
$\mathrm{Spearman}(s^{\text{early}},s^{\text{final}})$ over motifs for the same
molecule: an explanation that reorders the chemistry as training proceeds is
reporting optimisation noise as much as structure.

\paragraph{Calibration linkage and regimes.} Logits are temperature-scaled on
validation \cite{guo2017}. Per molecule we take the confidence
$\hat p=\max_c \mathrm{softmax}(f(G)/T)_c$ and assign a regime: \emph{confident
correct} ($\hat p\ge\tau$, correct), \emph{confident error} ($\hat p\ge\tau$,
wrong) --- the dangerous quadrant --- and \emph{borderline} ($\hat p<\tau$), with
$\tau=0.8$. Calibration linkage is the Spearman correlation between
$1-|\hat p - \mathbf{1}[\text{correct}]|$ and a reliability metric, testing
whether better-calibrated predictions carry more trustworthy attributions.
Every metric above is computed per molecule and aggregated with bootstrap
confidence intervals and paired Wilcoxon tests on shared molecules.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Grid.} The audit unit is a \emph{cell}: one (dataset $\times$ backbone
$\times$ attributor $\times$ split). We report \numNCells\ cells ---
\numNCls\ classification and \numNReg\ regression --- over \numNData\ datasets,
\numNBack\ backbones and \numNAttr\ attributors (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). The grid
is deliberately incomplete: it is not a full Cartesian product, because
attributors were added on top of already-trained backbones and a few
combinations are unsupported (PGExplainer's parametric mask is trained against
class logits and does not apply to regression).

\paragraph{Datasets.} Tier 1 provides ground truth: \textsc{SynthMotifs} (200
graphs) and \textsc{SynthMotifsXL} (600 graphs), generated offline with exact
node labels, and MUTAG (188 molecules) with the nitro proxy. Tier 2 and 3 are
real MPP tasks with no per-atom ground truth: BBBP, BACE, ClinTox, SIDER and
Tox21 from MoleculeNet \cite{wu2018moleculenet}; DILI and hERG from the
Therapeutics Data Commons \cite{huang2021tdc}; and ESOL, FreeSolv and
Lipophilicity as regression \cite{wu2018moleculenet}. Multi-task panels are
audited as single-task binary views (Tox21 NR-AR, SIDER task 0, ClinTox CT\_TOX).
TDC molecules are featurised with the same SMILES encoder as MoleculeNet so the
backbones are drop-in.

\paragraph{Backbones and attributors.} GINE \cite{xu2019gin,hu2020pretrain}, GCN
\cite{kipf2017}, GAT \cite{velickovic2018}, MPNN \cite{gilmer2017} and
AttentiveFP \cite{xiong2020}, all via PyTorch Geometric \cite{fey2019}.
Attributors: Integrated Gradients, Saliency, Input$\times$Gradient and Guided
Backpropagation through Captum \cite{kokhlikyan2020}, plus GNNExplainer and
PGExplainer.

\paragraph{Splits and budget.} The primary protocol is a Bemis--Murcko scaffold
split \cite{bemis1996} (80/10/10), made class-aware for imbalanced sets by moving
whole scaffold groups so that no fold is single-class --- no scaffold is split
across folds. Random splits are included as an in-distribution reference.
Results come from three CPU-tractable configurations: a broad matrix (30 epochs,
15 IG steps, 20 audited molecules per cell), a Tier-2/3 configuration (60 epochs,
25 IG steps, 30 molecules, inverse-frequency class weighting and AUC-based model
selection), and a ground-truth benchmark configuration (40 epochs, 25 IG steps,
the whole 120-molecule test fold of \textsc{SynthMotifsXL}). \textbf{This is a
reduced budget}, chosen so the audit runs on CPU; it bounds the precision of
every per-cell number and we treat it as a first-class limitation
(\S\ref{sec:limits}). Seeds, library versions and hardware are logged per run;
stochastic explainers are seeded per molecule so an explanation depends only on
(molecule, method). \numNCkpt\ trained checkpoints are committed, so the tables
regenerate without retraining.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Faithfulness does not imply correctness}
\label{sec:res-headline}

Figure~\ref{fig:scatter} plots every ground-truth-bearing cell in the
faithfulness--correctness plane. Across all \numNGtCells\ such cells the two axes
are statistically unrelated: Spearman $\rho=\numGlobalRho$
($p=\numGlobalP$). Knowing how faithful an attribution is tells you essentially
nothing about whether it recovers the right substructure.

The mechanism is visible in Table~\ref{tab:headline}. On MUTAG $\times$ GINE
under scaffold shift, the four gradient-family attributors occupy a narrow
faithfulness band --- $\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}$ from \numGradOccLo\ to
\numGradOccHi, a spread of \numGradOccSpread\ --- while their ground-truth AUROC
runs from \numGradGtLo\ to \numGradGtHi, a spread of \numGradGtSpread. Saliency
and Input$\times$Gradient sit near $0.03$--$0.04$, far \emph{below} chance, i.e.\
systematically anti-aligned with the nitro motif, yet they are among the most
faithful and score the highest Fidelity$+$ and characterisation values in the
table. Integrated Gradients and GNNExplainer recover the motif far better at
comparable faithfulness.

We are precise about what this does and does not show. Ranking these six
attributors by \emph{our} occlusion $\rho$ happens to put Integrated Gradients
on top, which is also the ground-truth-best method --- the argmax agrees. What
fails is the \emph{relationship}: faithfulness is nearly constant across methods
whose correctness differs by more than half an AUROC, so it cannot discriminate
between them, and the rank correlation over the six is $-0.03$
(Table~\ref{tab:selection}). The field-standard metrics are worse than
uninformative here: Fidelity$+$ ranks Guided Backpropagation first and
characterisation ranks Saliency first, with ground-truth AUROC of $0.112$ and
$0.026$ respectively.

\subsection{The dissociation is regime-dependent}
\label{sec:res-regime}

Faithfulness is not always uninformative --- and saying so is part of the
result. Table~\ref{tab:selection} and Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} contrast two
regimes. In distribution, on \textsc{SynthMotifsXL} with exact ground truth and
the full 120-molecule test fold, faithfulness tracks correctness well: rank
correlations are $+0.94$ ($p=0.005$) for occlusion $\rho$, $+0.83$ ($p=0.042$)
for Fidelity$+$ and $+0.77$ ($p=0.072$) for characterisation, and all three
selectors pick the ground-truth-best attributor. A faithfulness-only benchmark is
adequate in this regime.

Under scaffold shift on MUTAG the same three correlations invert in sign
($-0.03$, $-0.37$, $-0.49$), and Fidelity$+$ and characterisation actively
mis-select: they top-rank attributors whose ground-truth AUROC is $0.112$ and
$0.026$ against a best of $0.540$. Comparing the mis-selected attributor with the
ground-truth-best on the \emph{same} molecules, the per-molecule paired Wilcoxon
test rejects at $p<0.001$ for both.

The honest reading of the significance is mixed and we state it plainly. The
\emph{selection error} is well supported: it is a paired test over 20 molecules
with $p<10^{-4}$. The \emph{rank correlations} on the shift arm are not
individually significant --- they are computed over only six attributors, so
$p=0.957$, $0.468$ and $0.329$ --- and we therefore report the sign reversal as a
consistent pattern across three independent selectors rather than as an
individually significant statistic. The in-distribution correlations, over the
same six attributors, do reach significance for two of the three selectors.

\subsection{Reliability depends on the backbone}
\label{sec:res-backbone}

Holding the attributor fixed at Integrated Gradients and varying the backbone on
\textsc{SynthMotifs}, where ground truth is exact, localisation orders
GCN ($0.920$) $>$ AttentiveFP ($0.821$) $>$ MPNN ($0.807$) $>$ GINE ($0.742$)
$>$ GAT ($0.718$) (Figure~\ref{fig:backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:backbones}). The
spread is $0.202$ AUROC from the same attributor on the same task --- comparable
to the spread between attributors --- so ``which explainer'' and ``which
architecture'' are of similar importance to reliability.

Predictive accuracy does not buy explanation quality. GINE reaches test accuracy
$1.00$ and AUC $1.00$ on this task, and is fourth of five on localisation; GCN,
at accuracy $0.90$, localises best. AttentiveFP is the weakest classifier here
(accuracy $0.60$) yet is second on localisation. Model selection by predictive
performance therefore does not select for attribution reliability, which is an
argument for auditing the two separately.

\subsection{Cross-task patterns}
\label{sec:res-crosstask}

Figure~\ref{fig:heat} maps both metrics over the full grid and
Figure~\ref{fig:taskstrip} summarises faithfulness by task family. Faithfulness
is strongly dataset-dependent rather than a property of the attributor: on the
molecular classification sets, mean occlusion $\rho$ is
$\numClfMean$ over $\numClfN$ cells with $\numClfNeg$ of them negative, and the
per-dataset means range from clearly negative on BBBP to clearly positive on
hERG and SIDER. The same attributor can be faithful on one endpoint and
anti-faithful on another.

On exact ground truth the correctness picture is cleaner and instructive
(Table~\ref{tab:exactgt}): with $n=120$ molecules, the four gradient methods all
exceed $0.98$ AUROC, GNNExplainer sits at chance ($0.501$) and PGExplainer falls
below it ($0.269$). The perturbation-based explainers, which are the ones that
optimise an explicit mask, are the ones that fail to recover a motif the model
provably uses --- the model reaches $100\%$ test accuracy on this task.

\subsection{Negative and degenerate results}
\label{sec:res-negative}

We keep failures in the record.

\paragraph{A metric confound on regression.} $\numRegNeg$ of $\numRegN$
regression cells have negative occlusion $\rho$ (mean $\numRegMean$). The
tempting headline --- ``attributions are anti-faithful on regression'' --- is not
one we can support, and we flag the reason. Attributions are unsigned magnitudes,
whereas $\Delta_k$ for a regression head is a \emph{signed} change in the
predicted value: removing an important substructure can move a solubility
prediction in either direction. Correlating an unsigned quantity with a signed
one is not a well-posed faithfulness test, so these numbers are best read as
evidence that the occlusion protocol needs a signed or absolute-value variant for
regression, not as a property of the attributors. We report the cells because
they are part of the run, and mark the interpretation as unresolved.

\paragraph{Degenerate cells.} Imbalanced scaffold folds can collapse to a
majority-class predictor. BACE $\times$ GCN reaches test accuracy $0.133$ with
AUC $0.224$ --- worse than chance --- and BACE $\times$ GINE $0.433$ accuracy at
AUC $0.434$; attributions of a model that has not learned the task are not
meaningful, and we exclude such cells from the interpretive claims above while
leaving them in the released tables. Conversely, high accuracy can be an artefact
of imbalance: Tox21 $\times$ GINE shows accuracy $1.000$ at AUC $0.827$ on a
$\sim\!4\%$-positive endpoint, which is why we report AUC alongside accuracy
everywhere.

\paragraph{Missing entries.} Correctness is undefined for the ten datasets with
no per-atom ground truth, and several attributor--dataset combinations were not
run. Both appear as \texttt{--}. We do not impute, and we do not report a
grid-wide mean over a metric that exists for only \numNGtCells\ of \numNCells\
cells.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the audit buys.} The practical consequence of
\S\ref{sec:res-headline}--\S\ref{sec:res-regime} is that a faithfulness score
should not be reported as if it certified an explanation. In distribution it
behaves like a reasonable proxy for correctness; under the scaffold shift that
defines realistic MPP evaluation, it stops predicting correctness and can invert.
Since ground truth is exactly what is unavailable in the deployment regime, the
usable recommendation is not ``measure correctness instead'' but
``calibrate your faithfulness metric on a task where ground truth \emph{is}
available, in the same shift regime, before trusting it'' --- which is what the
\textsc{SynthMotifs} control is for.

\paragraph{How attributions should be reported.} Our results argue for three
changes in practice. First, report attributor \emph{and} backbone: the spread
across backbones ($0.202$ AUROC) is comparable to the spread across attributors,
so an explanation is a property of the pair. Second, report the split regime, and
report an in-distribution reference alongside the shifted one, since the same
metric can behave oppositely in the two. Third, report negative controls: an
attributor scoring $0.026$ against a chemically motivated mask is making a
falsifiable claim, and that is far more informative than a fidelity number with
no reference point.

\paragraph{Why reliability is regime-dependent.} A faithfulness metric asks what
the model uses; a ground-truth metric asks what the chemistry says. The two
coincide only when the model has actually learned the causal substructure. In
distribution on a synthetic task with an injected motif, that condition holds ---
the model reaches $100\%$ accuracy using the motif --- and faithfulness and
correctness agree. Under scaffold shift on real chemistry the condition need not
hold: a model can rely on scaffold-correlated shortcuts that generalise poorly,
and a faithful attribution will then faithfully report the shortcut. This also
explains why the perturbation-based explainers, which optimise a mask against the
model's own output, can be at or below chance on a task the model solves: they
are optimising fidelity to the model, which is not the same objective as
recovering the motif.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Reduced budget.} Every number here comes from CPU-tractable
configurations: 30--60 training epochs, 15--25 integration steps, and 20--30
audited molecules for most cells (120 for the exact-ground-truth control). Per-cell
estimates are correspondingly noisy, and the bootstrap intervals we compute are
wide. The pipeline supports a larger configuration; we do not report it, and no
claim here should be read as a converged measurement.

\paragraph{Single split, single seed.} Each cell is one scaffold split at one
seed. We therefore make claims about \emph{patterns across many cells} rather than
about individual cell values, and the ordering of two adjacent backbones (GINE
$0.742$ vs.\ GAT $0.718$) should not be over-read.

\paragraph{Ground truth is scarce and partly a proxy.} Correctness is measurable
on three of \numNData\ datasets. MUTAG's mask is a chemically motivated nitro
proxy, not annotator labels: it encodes a hypothesis about mutagenicity, and an
attributor could in principle be right about a model that is right for a
different reason. The exact-ground-truth results come from synthetic graphs,
which are not chemistry. The shift arm of our headline analysis rests on MUTAG at
$n=20$ molecules; it is significant as a paired test, but it is one dataset.

\paragraph{Underpowered rank correlations.} The selector correlations in
Table~\ref{tab:selection} are computed over six attributors. Four of six do not
reach $p<0.05$, and we lean on the paired per-molecule tests and on the
consistency of the sign pattern instead.

\paragraph{Regression faithfulness is confounded.} As set out in
\S\ref{sec:res-negative}, the unsigned-attribution/signed-$\Delta$ mismatch makes
the regression $\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}$ values uninterpretable as faithfulness. This
is a defect in the protocol, not a finding.

\paragraph{Scope.} SubgraphX \cite{yuan2021subgraphx} and the GraphXAI
\cite{agarwal2023} synthetic generator could not be installed in our environment
(they depend on a pre-2.5 PyTorch Geometric extension stack with no wheel for the
installed PyTorch), so both are absent rather than negative results. Our
comparison to GraphFramEx is a faithful reimplementation of its characterisation
score within our pipeline, not a run of the authors' code.

\section{Conclusion}

\textsc{MolSanity} audits attribution reliability for molecular GNNs rather than
proposing another attributor. Over \numNCells\ cells we find that faithfulness
and ground-truth correctness are uncorrelated in aggregate
($\rho=\numGlobalRho$), that the relationship between them is positive
in-distribution and inverts under scaffold shift, that field-standard fidelity
metrics consequently mis-select attributors in the shifted regime with
per-molecule $p<0.001$, and that reliability depends on the backbone about as
much as on the attributor while being unrelated to predictive accuracy. The
practical implication is narrow and, we think, well supported: a faithfulness
score is not a certificate of correctness, and should be reported with the
backbone, the split regime and a ground-truth reference point attached.
