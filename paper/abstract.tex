\textbf{Abstract.}
Feature attributions are routinely used to justify molecular graph neural
network (GNN) predictions to chemists, yet they are almost never audited for
\emph{reliability}: existing evaluation frameworks ask whether an explanation
is faithful to the model, not whether the model's explanation can be trusted,
and not where it stops being trustworthy under the scaffold shift that
characterises real drug-discovery workflows. We present \textbf{MolSanity}, a
reliability-audit framework that wraps canonical attribution implementations
(Captum, PyTorch Geometric, RDKit) rather than proposing a new attributor, and
scores every (dataset × backbone × attributor) cell on six axes: motif-native
coherence, occlusion--attribution faithfulness, ground-truth localisation
where node labels exist, cross-checkpoint stability, calibration linkage, and
confidence/correctness regime stratification. Across \nCellsDone{} committed
audit cells spanning \nDatasetsDone{} datasets, \nBackbonesDone{} backbones and
\nAttributorsDone{} attributors, the audit shows that \emph{faithfulness is not
correctness}: in \nFaithfulButWrong{} of the \nGtOccCells{} cells where a
node-level ground truth exists, an attribution that tracks the model
positively (occlusion $\rho>0$) is nonetheless anti-aligned with the true motif
(GT AUROC $<0.5$). On MUTAG under a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split, Saliency and
Input$\times$Gradient are not distinguishable from Integrated Gradients in
faithfulness ($\rho=\mutagSalOcc{}$, $\mutagIxGOcc{}$ vs.\ $\mutagIGOcc{}$;
paired Wilcoxon $p=\mutagIGvsSalP{}$ and $p=\mutagIGvsIxGP{}$) while localising
the mutagenic nitro motif at GT AUROC $\mutagSalGt{}$ and $\mutagIxGGt{}$
against IG's $\mutagIGGt{}$. Ranking attributors by field-standard fidelity
recovers the ground-truth-best method in distribution ($\rho$(faithfulness,
truth) $=\xlRhoOccspearman{}$--$\xlRhoCharacterization{}$, no mismatch,
$n=\xlNMol{}$) but fails under shift, where GraphFramEx characterisation
selects the worst-localising attributor of six (paired Wilcoxon
$p=\shiftPCharacterization{}$) and the rank correlation collapses to
$\shiftRhoCharacterization{}$. The attributor ordering itself reverses between
regimes ($\rho=\attrRankRho{}$ across six attributors), and the backbone
ordering does not transfer between datasets ($\rho=\backboneRankRho{}$). We
therefore argue that an attribution should never be reported without the regime
it was audited in. Results reflect the latest committed run:
\nCellsPlanDone{}/\nCellsPlanned{} cells of the planned grid are complete, the
remainder are marked pending and left blank --- never imputed. Code, configs,
checkpoints and every number in this paper are in the repository.
