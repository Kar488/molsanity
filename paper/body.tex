\section{Introduction}

A medicinal chemist is shown a graph neural network's prediction that a
candidate is hERG-active, together with a heat map over its atoms. The heat map
concentrates on a piperidine nitrogen; the chemist finds that plausible, and the
series is deprioritised. Nothing in that workflow ever established that the
highlighted atoms are the atoms the model actually used, that they are the atoms
that matter chemically, or that the highlighting behaves the same way on the
next scaffold. Each of those is a separate question, and the answers can come
apart.

They come apart in a specific, measurable way. A saliency map can be
\emph{faithful} --- the atoms it ranks highest really are the atoms whose
removal moves the model's output most --- and simultaneously \emph{wrong} ---
those atoms are not the substructure that determines the property. On MUTAG
under a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split, we measure Saliency at occlusion
faithfulness $\rho=\mutagSalOcc{}$, not distinguishable from Integrated
Gradients at $\mutagIGOcc{}$ on the same molecules (paired Wilcoxon
$p=\mutagIGvsSalP{}$, $n=\mutagIGvsSalN{}$), while its agreement with the mutagenic
nitro motif is GT AUROC $\mutagSalGt{}$ against IG's $\mutagIGGt{}$ --- a gap of
more than half an AUROC point at equal faithfulness, in the direction that
matters (chance is $0.5$; below $0.5$ the attribution is systematically
\emph{anti}-aligned with the true motif). A benchmark that scores only
faithfulness rates both methods as fine.

This is not an argument that faithfulness metrics are wrong. It is an argument
that they are answering a different question from the one a chemist is asking,
and that the gap between the two questions widens exactly where molecular
property prediction operates: out of distribution, on scaffolds the model has
not seen. Molecular property prediction is deployed under scaffold shift almost
by construction --- the point of a screening model is to score series the
training set does not contain --- and the standard evaluation protocol for
explanations does not stratify by that shift at all.

\paragraph{Contributions} This paper presents \textbf{MolSanity}, a
reliability-audit framework for feature attributions on molecular GNNs. It is
not a new attribution method: it wraps the canonical implementations
(Captum~\cite{captum}, PyTorch Geometric~\cite{pyg}, RDKit~\cite{rdkit}) and
audits them. Specifically:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item We define a six-axis audit over a motif-native (RDKit) decomposition:
  coherence, occlusion--attribution faithfulness, ground-truth localisation,
  cross-checkpoint stability, calibration linkage and confidence/correctness
  regime stratification, with field-standard Fidelity$\pm$, sparsity, the
  GraphFramEx characterisation score~\cite{graphframex} and the PyG/DIG
  unfaithfulness metric~\cite{dig} computed on the \emph{same} molecules for
  direct comparability (\nameref{sec:framework}).
\item We run it over \nCellsDone{} committed (dataset × backbone × attributor ×
  split) cells covering \nDatasetsDone{} datasets, \nBackbonesDone{} GNN
  backbones and \nAttributorsDone{} attributors, and show that faithfulness and
  correctness dissociate: \nFaithfulButWrong{} of the \nGtOccCells{} cells that
  have a node-level ground truth are positively faithful yet anti-aligned with
  the truth.
\item We quantify the consequence for benchmarking practice. Ranking
  attributors by a faithfulness/fidelity metric --- what current evaluation
  frameworks emit --- recovers the ground-truth-best attributor
  in-distribution, but under scaffold shift the field-standard scores select an
  attributor the ground truth ranks last (paired Wilcoxon
  $p=\shiftPCharacterization{}$), and the faithfulness--truth rank correlation
  collapses from $+\xlRhoCharacterization{}$ to $\shiftRhoCharacterization{}$.
\item We show the reliability ordering is not a property of the attributor
  alone: the attributor ranking reverses between the synthetic and the
  molecular regime ($\rho=\attrRankRho{}$, $n=\attrRankRhoN{}$ attributors), and
  the backbone ranking does not transfer between datasets
  ($\rho=\backboneRankRho{}$, $n=\backboneRankRhoN{}$ backbones). No attributor
  and no backbone is universally reliable.
\item We report the negatives with the positives: degenerate cells, cells where
  the correctness axis simply does not exist, a systematically anti-faithful
  regression regime, and dependencies that could not be installed. Every number
  in this paper is generated from committed artifacts by scripts in
  \texttt{paper/figs/}; cells that have not finished are blank, not imputed.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope of this draft} The grid is still running. This version reports
the \nCellsDone{} cells committed as of the latest run;
\nCellsPlanDone{}/\nCellsPlanned{} cells of the planned sweep
(\texttt{configs/full.yaml}) are complete. \emph{\nameref{sec:setup}} states
exactly what is missing, and \emph{\nameref{sec:limits}} treats it as a
limitation rather than a footnote.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Attribution methods} The attributors audited here are the standard
ones. Gradient-family methods --- Saliency~\cite{saliency}, Input×Gradient,
Guided Backpropagation~\cite{guidedbp} and Integrated
Gradients~\cite{ig} --- are model-agnostic and cheap. Graph-specific methods
learn a mask over the input: GNNExplainer~\cite{gnnexplainer} optimises a soft
edge/feature mask per instance, PGExplainer~\cite{pgexplainer} amortises that
into a parametric mask network, and SubgraphX~\cite{subgraphx} searches
connected subgraphs with Monte-Carlo tree search under a Shapley objective.
Graph analogues of CAM/Grad-CAM were introduced by Pope et
al.~\cite{gradcamgraph}; Yuan et al.~\cite{gnnsurvey} survey the space. We wrap,
never reimplement.

\paragraph{Evaluating explanations} Two evaluation traditions exist.
\emph{Faithfulness} measures whether the explanation tracks the model:
Fidelity$\pm$ and sparsity~\cite{gnnsurvey}, the GraphFramEx characterisation
score that combines them~\cite{graphframex}, and the unfaithfulness metric
shipped with DIG/PyG~\cite{dig}. \emph{Correctness} measures whether the
explanation recovers a known rationale, which requires ground-truth
substructures --- typically synthetic~\cite{graphxai}. The tension between the
two is known: Faber et al.~\cite{faber} argue that comparing against ground
truth is misleading when the trained model does not use that rationale, and
Sanchez-Lengeling et al.~\cite{sanchezlengeling} built the first systematic
attribution benchmark for molecular GNNs on exactly this basis. Outside graphs,
sanity checks~\cite{sanitychecks} and retraining-based
benchmarks~\cite{roar} made a parallel case that plausible-looking attributions
can be uninformative. MolSanity's position is that neither axis subsumes the
other and that the \emph{joint} distribution --- and how it moves under
scaffold shift --- is the object of interest.

\paragraph{Frameworks, and the gap} GraphXAI~\cite{graphxai} supplies synthetic
generators with exact ground truth and an evaluation library;
GraphFramEx~\cite{graphframex} systematises fidelity-style evaluation across
explainers and tasks; DIG~\cite{dig} packages methods and metrics; MolFaith
\cite{molfaith} evaluates feature attributions on molecular property models
under fidelity-based metrics. Table~\ref{tab:related} places MolSanity against
them. The claim is deliberately narrow: no individual row of that table is new.
What is new is the combination --- ground-truth localisation \emph{and}
faithfulness \emph{and} cross-checkpoint stability \emph{and} calibration
linkage, computed over a motif-native decomposition, stratified by scaffold
shift, across five backbones, with the field-standard metrics recomputed on the
same molecules so the comparison is head-to-head rather than cross-paper.

\section{The MolSanity audit}
\label{sec:framework}

\paragraph{Notation} A molecule is a graph $G=(V,E)$ with node features
$X\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|\times d}$ and edge features. A model $f$ maps $G$ to a
logit vector (classification) or a scalar (regression); $t$ denotes the
explained target. An attributor $A$ produces node attributions
$a=A(f,G,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|}$. RDKit decomposes $G$ into motifs
$\mathcal{M}=\{M_1,\dots,M_K\}$, $M_k\subseteq V$, from SSSR rings, the
Bemis--Murcko scaffold~\cite{murcko} and fragment decomposition; the motif score
is $s_k=\sum_{v\in M_k}\max(a_v,0)$. Working at motif rather than atom
granularity is what makes the audit chemically legible: a chemist reasons about
a nitro group, not about atom 14.

\paragraph{Coherence} Does the attribution concentrate mass sensibly? We report
the Gini coefficient of $a$, the mass in the top 20\% of atoms, the largest
connected-component fraction among salient atoms, and the motif top-1 share
$\max_k s_k/\sum_k s_k$. Coherence is necessary but never sufficient: an
attribution can be perfectly concentrated on the wrong motif.

\paragraph{Occlusion faithfulness} For each motif we zero its atoms' features
through the model's node-mask multiplier and measure the drop in the target
logit, $\Delta_k=f_t(G)-f_t(G\setminus M_k)$. Faithfulness is
$\rho=\mathrm{Spearman}(s,\Delta)$ over the motifs of a molecule, plus the
top-1 agreement indicator $\mathbb{1}[\arg\max_k s_k=\arg\max_k \Delta_k]$.
\textbf{Higher $\rho$ means more faithful}: the attributor ranks motifs the way
occlusion does. $\rho<0$ means the attributor ranks motifs in the reverse order
of their causal effect on the model --- worse than an uninformative
explanation. For regression, $\Delta$ is taken in output space rather than
probability space. We also compute Fidelity+ (target-probability drop when the
salient atoms are removed; higher is better), Fidelity$-$ (drop when the
non-salient atoms are removed; lower is better), sparsity, the GraphFramEx
characterisation score (their weighted harmonic mean~\cite{graphframex}) and
the PyG/DIG unfaithfulness metric~\cite{dig}, on the same molecules and the
same masks.

\paragraph{Ground-truth localisation} Where a node-level ground-truth mask
$g\in\{0,1\}^{|V|}$ exists, correctness is the AUROC/AUPRC of the normalised
$a$ against $g$. Chance is $0.5$; below $0.5$ the attribution is anti-aligned.
When $g$ is degenerate (all-0 or all-1) the metric is undefined and reported as
such --- never as $0$. Only the synthetic sets carry exact $g$; MUTAG carries a
chemically motivated nitro/aromatic-nitro SMARTS proxy after Debnath et
al.~\cite{mutag}, and we label it a proxy everywhere. No molecular dataset in
the sweep ships per-atom explanation labels, which is precisely why the
synthetic anchor is needed.

\paragraph{Cross-checkpoint stability} An attribution that changes when the
model is retrained a little is a weak basis for a chemistry decision. We keep an
early checkpoint of the same run and report
$\mathrm{Spearman}(s^{\text{early}},s^{\text{final}})$ per molecule. This axis
is absent from the frameworks in Table~\ref{tab:related}.

\paragraph{Calibration linkage and regime stratification} Predictions are
temperature-scaled on validation~\cite{calibration} and we report test expected
calibration error (ECE) alongside the attribution metrics, so a cell's
explanation quality can be read next to whether its confidence means anything.
Per molecule, the audit assigns a regime from calibrated confidence and
correctness --- \emph{confident-correct}, \emph{confident-error}
(the dangerous one) and \emph{borderline} --- and stratifies the reliability
metrics by it, together with the correlation between per-molecule calibration
error and reliability. The per-molecule records that back this stratification
are written to \texttt{artifacts/audit/} and are not committed to the
repository, so this draft reports cell-level aggregates only; the
regime-stratified analysis is deferred to the completed run
(\nameref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Aggregation} Cells are compared with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests on shared molecules and bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over
molecules. $p$-values are reported unadjusted, and we treat them as descriptive
given the reduced per-cell molecule counts.

\paragraph{Engineering} The pipeline is staged and idempotent: each stage writes
a \texttt{.done} marker keyed to a content hash of its config, every cell is
wrapped so a failure is logged and the run continues, and trained checkpoints
are committed so the audit reproduces without retraining. Seeds, library
versions, git revision and hardware are recorded in a per-run manifest.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets} Tier-1 provides the ground-truth anchor:
\textbf{SynthMotifs} and its larger twin \textbf{SynthMotifsXL}, generated
offline (Barab\'asi--Albert base plus house/cycle motifs) with \emph{exact}
node ground truth, and \textbf{MUTAG}~\cite{mutag,tudataset} with the nitro
proxy. Tier-2 is MoleculeNet~\cite{moleculenet}: \textbf{BBBP} and
\textbf{BACE} (classification), \textbf{ESOL}, \textbf{FreeSolv} and
\textbf{Lipophilicity} (regression). Tier-3 covers
chemistry-meets-medicine endpoints: \textbf{ClinTox}, \textbf{SIDER} and
\textbf{Tox21} as single-task binary views of the MoleculeNet panels, and
\textbf{DILI} (drug-induced liver injury) and \textbf{hERG} (cardiotoxicity)
via the Therapeutics Data Commons~\cite{tdc}. TDC sets are featurised with the
same PyG encoding as MoleculeNet so the backbones are drop-in. BA-2Motifs and
ShapeGGen~\cite{graphxai} are attempted and gracefully skipped: the former's
download returns HTTP 403 in this environment, the latter's install depends on
the pre-2.5 PyG compiled extensions. Credential-gated resources are out of
scope and are never worked around.

\paragraph{Backbones} GINE~\cite{gin,pretraingnn}, GCN~\cite{gcn},
GAT~\cite{gat}, MPNN~\cite{mpnn} and AttentiveFP~\cite{attentivefp}, sharing a
pooling head, calibration and splits.

\paragraph{Attributors} Integrated Gradients, Saliency, Guided
Backpropagation and Input×Gradient through Captum~\cite{captum}; GNNExplainer and
PGExplainer through PyTorch Geometric~\cite{pyg}. SubgraphX~\cite{subgraphx}
requires DIG, which installs but cannot import in this environment (no
\texttt{torch\_\-sparse} wheel at the pinned torch version), so it is left
blocked rather than approximated. PGExplainer is classification-only by construction:
its mask network trains against class logits, so it is absent from the
regression cells.

\paragraph{Splits and budget} The primary split is a deterministic, class-aware
Bemis--Murcko scaffold split~\cite{murcko} (whole scaffold groups move together,
so there is no leakage, but every fold is guaranteed to contain every class),
with a random split as the in-distribution reference. Imbalanced classification
uses inverse-frequency class weighting and AUC-based model selection. All
committed cells were produced at a \emph{reduced} CPU budget --- 30--60 training
epochs, 15--25 IG steps, and 20--30 audited molecules per cell (120 for the
powered in-distribution arm) --- not at the publication budget defined in
\texttt{configs/full.yaml} (150 epochs, 50 IG steps, 100 molecules per cell).
This matters for absolute numbers and for statistical power, and we flag it
wherever it could change a conclusion.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Faithfulness is not correctness}

\input{generated/tab_tier1}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_faith_correct.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The two axes come apart, and for most cells only one of them
exists.} \textbf{(a)} Every committed cell that carries a node-level ground
truth ($n=\nGtOccCells{}$), plotted as occlusion faithfulness against
ground-truth localisation; colour distinguishes exact synthetic ground truth
from the MUTAG motif proxy, marker distinguishes attributor. The shaded band is
GT AUROC $<0.5$: attributions there are anti-aligned with the true motif.
\nFaithfulButWrong{} cells sit in the lower-right quadrant --- positively
faithful to the model and anti-aligned with the truth. \textbf{(b)} The
remaining \nNoGtCells{} committed cells, on real molecular datasets, have no
published per-atom labels: only the horizontal axis is measurable for them.
Regression cells (median $\rho=\medRegressionOcc{}$) sit almost entirely at

\label{fig:faithcorrect}
\end{figure*}

Table~\ref{tab:tier1} lists every committed cell in which attribution
\emph{correctness} can be measured at all, and Figure~\ref{fig:faithcorrect}
plots the joint distribution. Two observations organise everything that
follows.

First, the axes are close to orthogonal in the region that matters. Of the
\nGtOccCells{} cells with both metrics defined, \nAntiAligned{} are anti-aligned
with the ground truth (GT AUROC $<0.5$), and \nFaithfulButWrong{} of those are
simultaneously positively faithful to the model. That combination --- the
lower-right quadrant of Figure~\ref{fig:faithcorrect}a --- is the failure mode
a faithfulness-only audit cannot see, because from the model's point of view
nothing is wrong.

Second, the MUTAG scaffold-split block of Table~\ref{tab:tier1} shows the
phenomenon at fixed dataset, fixed backbone and fixed split, so the only thing
varying is the attributor. Saliency, Input×Gradient and Integrated Gradients
have occlusion faithfulness $\mutagSalOcc{}$, $\mutagIxGOcc{}$ and
$\mutagIGOcc{}$ --- a spread of $\mutagGradOccSpread{}$, and neither gradient method is
distinguishable from IG on paired per-molecule tests ($p=\mutagIGvsSalP{}$ and
$p=\mutagIGvsIxGP{}$) --- while their agreement with the nitro motif is $\mutagSalGt{}$, $\mutagIxGGt{}$ and $\mutagIGGt{}$. Guided
Backpropagation behaves like the other two gradient methods ($\mutagGBPOcc{}$
faithful, $\mutagGBPGt{}$ correct). The two mask-learning methods land
elsewhere: GNNExplainer is less faithful ($\mutagGNNEOcc{}$) but far better
localised ($\mutagGNNEGt{}$), and PGExplainer likewise ($\mutagPGEOcc{}$,
$\mutagPGEGt{}$). Reading the faithfulness column alone would rank the gradient
family at the top; reading the ground-truth column reverses that. Both columns
are computed on the same 20 molecules and the same trained model.

We are careful about what this does and does not license. MUTAG's mask is a
motif proxy, not annotator ground truth, and $n=20$; on its own this is a
suggestive case study. Its role in the argument is to show the phenomenon on a
real molecular dataset, with the synthetic sets --- where the mask is exact ---
carrying the load in the next section.

\subsection{A fidelity-only ranking picks the wrong attributor under shift}

\input{generated/tab_selection}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_dissociation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{In distribution, faithfulness predicts correctness; under
scaffold shift it does not.} Ground-truth localisation per attributor, on
shared molecules, with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals; annotations mark
which attributor each faithfulness-style ranking would have selected.
\textbf{(a)} \xlDataset{} (exact node ground truth, random split,
$n=\xlNMol{}$): all three rankings pick the ground-truth-best attributor.
\textbf{(b)} MUTAG (motif-proxy ground truth, scaffold split, $n=\shiftNMol{}$):
Fidelity+ picks \shiftPickFidelityplus{} (GT AUROC $\shiftPickGtFidelityplus{}$)
and the GraphFramEx characterisation score picks \shiftPickCharacterization{}
(GT AUROC $\shiftPickGtCharacterization{}$), against a ground-truth-best of
\shiftGtBest{} at $\shiftPickGtOccspearman{}$. Rank correlations between each
faithfulness metric and ground truth are given below each panel.}
\label{fig:dissociation}
\end{figure*}

If faithfulness and correctness dissociate, then ranking attributors by a
faithfulness metric --- which is what an evaluation framework does when no
ground truth is available --- can select the wrong method. We test this
directly on cells where all six attributors are audited on the same molecules
(Table~\ref{tab:selection}, Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}).

In distribution, faithfulness is an adequate proxy. On \xlDataset{} with exact
node ground truth and $n=\xlNMol{}$ molecules on a random split, all three
ranking metrics --- our occlusion $\rho$, Fidelity+ and the GraphFramEx
characterisation score --- select \xlGtBest{}, which is also the
ground-truth-best attributor, and the rank correlation between metric and truth
across the six attributors is $\xlRhoOccspearman{}$, $\xlRhoFidelityplus{}$ and
$\xlRhoCharacterization{}$ respectively. There are \xlNMismatch{} mismatches.
A fidelity-only benchmark is defensible here.

Under scaffold shift on MUTAG, it is not. The ground truth ranks
\shiftGtBest{} best ($\shiftPickGtOccspearman{}$). Fidelity+ instead ranks
\shiftPickFidelityplus{} first, whose ground-truth localisation is
$\shiftPickGtFidelityplus{}$; the characterisation score ranks
\shiftPickCharacterization{} first, at $\shiftPickGtCharacterization{}$ --- the
worst of the six. Both selections differ from the ground-truth-best attributor
on paired per-molecule GT AUROC with median gaps $\shiftGapFidelityplus{}$ and
$\shiftGapCharacterization{}$ (paired Wilcoxon $p=\shiftPFidelityplus{}$ and
$p=\shiftPCharacterization{}$, $n=\shiftNMol{}$ shared molecules). The rank
correlation between faithfulness and truth collapses:
$\shiftRhoFidelityplus{}$ for Fidelity+ and $\shiftRhoCharacterization{}$ for
characterisation, i.e.\ mildly \emph{negative}.

One detail must not be glossed. MolSanity's own occlusion $\rho$ does
\emph{not} mis-select on this cell --- it ranks \shiftPickOccspearman{} first,
which is the ground-truth-best --- but its rank correlation with the truth is
$\shiftRhoOccspearman{}$, indistinguishable from zero. It gets the top of the
ranking right and the rest of the ranking wrong. The honest conclusion is not
that our faithfulness metric is better than the field-standard ones; it is that
\emph{no} faithfulness metric in this experiment carries usable information
about correctness under shift, and one of the three happened to land on the
right winner.

The design of this experiment is also its main weakness, and we state it here
rather than in the limitations only: the in-distribution arm and the shift arm
are \emph{different datasets} (\xlDataset{}, synthetic and exact, versus MUTAG,
molecular and proxy) as well as different splits. Regime and dataset are
therefore confounded. The comparison shows that a fidelity-only ranking is
adequate in one setting and misleading in another; it does not isolate scaffold
shift as the sole cause. Disentangling the two requires the pending
random-split arm on the molecular datasets (\nameref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{The attributor ranking is regime-dependent}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig_heatmaps.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Reliability is a property of the (attributor, dataset) pair,
not of the attributor.} All committed GINE cells at each dataset's primary
split. \textbf{(a)} Occlusion faithfulness; blue $=$ anti-faithful.
\textbf{(b)} Ground-truth localisation; the grey block is not a low score but
an \emph{unmeasurable} one --- no molecular dataset here publishes per-atom
labels. ``---'' marks an audited cell whose metric is undefined, ``$\cdot$'' a
cell that has not been audited. The attributor ordering in the three
ground-truth columns of (b) is not preserved between them
($\rho=\attrRankRho{}$ between SynthMotifs and MUTAG).}
\label{fig:heatmaps}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmaps} lays the two axes out over the audited grid. On
SynthMotifs, where the model reaches 100\% test accuracy and provably uses the
planted motif, the gradient family localises it almost perfectly --- Saliency
$\synthSalGt{}$, Guided Backpropagation $\synthGBPGt{}$, Input×Gradient
$\synthIxGGt{}$ --- while Integrated Gradients trails at $\synthIGGt{}$,
GNNExplainer sits at chance ($\synthGNNEGt{}$) and PGExplainer below it
($\synthPGEGt{}$). On MUTAG, as above, that ordering is close to inverted. The
Spearman correlation between the two GT orderings over the six shared
attributors is $\attrRankRho{}$, and between SynthMotifsXL and MUTAG it is
$\attrRankRhoXL{}$.

Two mechanisms plausibly contribute, and our data cannot separate them. The
synthetic task is one where the model's rationale and the ground-truth
rationale coincide by construction, so any attributor that is faithful is
thereby also correct; MUTAG is a task where the model may key on something
correlated with, but not identical to, the nitro group --- the situation Faber
et al.~\cite{faber} identify as making ground-truth comparison hazardous. That
is a reason to report both axes rather than to pick one: an attributor's
apparent quality is inherited partly from whether the model under audit is
using the rationale the ground truth encodes.

The practical reading is narrow and defensible: a recommendation of the form
``use method $X$ for molecular GNNs'' is not supported by any cell in this
matrix, because the ranking that supports it is regime-specific.

\subsection{Backbone dependence}


Holding the attributor fixed at Integrated Gradients and varying the backbone
(Figure~\ref{fig:backbone}) gives a spread of $\synthIgSpread{}$ AUROC on
SynthMotifs (\synthIgBestBackbone{} $\synthGCNIgGt{}$ down to
\synthIgWorstBackbone{} $\synthGATIgGt{}$) and $\mutagIgSpread{}$ on MUTAG
(\mutagIgBestBackbone{} $\mutagGINEIgGt{}$ down to \mutagIgWorstBackbone{}
$\mutagGATIgGt{}$). Both spreads are comparable to the spread across
attributors on the same dataset. The orderings do not transfer:
$\rho=\backboneRankRho{}$ over the \backboneRankRhoN{} shared backbones. On
MUTAG, four of the five backbones put IG's localisation below chance; only GINE
is above it.

AttentiveFP on MUTAG is worth singling out because it fails on both axes at
once: occlusion faithfulness $\mutagAttentiveFPIgOcc{}$ --- strongly
\emph{anti}-faithful, the attribution ranks motifs in almost exactly the reverse
of their causal order --- with GT AUROC $\mutagAttentiveFPIgGt{}$, on a model
whose test ROC-AUC is the highest of the five backbones (Table~\ref{tab:tier1}).
Predictive performance and explanation reliability are not the same axis, and
this cell is the clearest demonstration in the matrix.

Table~\ref{tab:regression} and Tables~\ref{tab:molecular}--\ref{tab:molecularb}

\paragraph{Regression is systematically anti-faithful} Across
\nRegressionCells{} committed regression cells, \nNegRegression{} have negative
occlusion faithfulness, with median $\medRegressionOcc{}$; the sole exception is
Lipophilicity under GAT. The same cells have well-behaved predictive
performance ($R^2$ from $\esolRtwoMin{}$ to $\esolRtwoMax{}$ on ESOL across
\nEsolBackbones{} backbones). Two
readings are available and we cannot yet separate them: either attributions on
these regression models genuinely invert the causal ordering of motifs, or the
occlusion operator --- zeroing node features and reading the shift in a scalar
output --- is not a well-posed counterfactual in output space, where masking
atoms moves predictions toward a pooled mean regardless of which atoms are
masked. The negative Fidelity$\pm$ values in Table~\ref{tab:regression} are
consistent with the second reading. We therefore report the regression numbers
but do not build the headline on them, and we flag the probability-space
definition of Fidelity$\pm$ as not transferring cleanly to regression.

\paragraph{Coherence is high nearly everywhere, and says little} Motif top-1
share lies between $\motifTopMin{}$ and $\motifTopMax{}$ across the
\nMolecularCells{} molecular cells of Tables~\ref{tab:regression}--%
\ref{tab:molecularb}, with \nMotifInBand{} of them inside a narrow
$0.6$--$0.9$ band, largely independent of whether the same cell is faithful or
anti-faithful. Concentration on a single
motif is easy to achieve and, on this evidence, close to uninformative about
reliability. It belongs in the audit as a descriptive statistic, not as a
quality score.

\paragraph{Stability is high but not uniform} Median cross-checkpoint stability
over \nStabilityCells{} cells is $\medStability{}$, with a minimum of
$\minStability{}$. By attributor, GNNExplainer is the most stable
(median $\stabGNNE{}$, $n=\nStabGNNE{}$) and PGExplainer the least
($\stabPGE{}$, $n=\nStabPGE{}$), with Integrated Gradients at $\stabIG{}$
($n=\nStabIG{}$); the per-attributor counts are unequal, so these medians are
descriptive rather than a controlled comparison.


Table~\ref{tab:paired} shows why per-cell molecule counts matter. On the
powered in-distribution cell ($n=\xlNMol{}$) the paired tests separate the
attributor families decisively; on the shift cell ($n=\shiftNMol{}$) most
contrasts are indistinguishable. A framework that ranks attributors on
faithfulness at these sample sizes is often ranking noise --- an independent
reason not to treat a fidelity leaderboard as a method recommendation.

\subsection{Negative and degenerate results}

We record the cells that did not work, because they carry information.

\paragraph{A broken model can be faithfully explained} The lowest-AUC cell in
the matrix is \worstAucDataset{}~×~\worstAucBackbone{}, with test accuracy
$\worstAucAcc{}$ and ROC-AUC $\worstAuc{}$ --- substantially \emph{worse} than
chance, i.e.\ an anti-predictive model. Its attributions are among the most
faithful in the entire classification block (occlusion $\rho=\worstAucOcc{}$,
characterisation $\worstAucChar{}$). Faithfulness is a model-relative property:
it certifies that the explanation describes the model, and says nothing about
whether the model is worth describing. Any reliability report that omits the
predictive metrics can be gamed by exactly this cell.

\paragraph{Majority-class collapse} Four rows in Table~\ref{tab:molecularb}
report test accuracy $\geq0.99$ with ROC-AUC below $0.9$ --- all Tox21, a
single-task view in which positives are a small minority. Accuracy is the wrong headline there and
we report AUC alongside it throughout. Under the reduced budget, scaffold splits
on imbalanced sets could produce near-single-class test folds; the class-aware
split and inverse-frequency weighting used for the Tier-2/3 cells are what keep
these cells interpretable, and the earlier unweighted pass is retained in the
repository rather than deleted.

\paragraph{Calibration varies more than accuracy} Test ECE across the
classification matrix has median $\medEce{}$ and reaches $\maxEce{}$. Since the
audit's regime stratification keys off calibrated confidence, cells at the top
of that range cannot support a confident-error analysis at all --- a
prerequisite the audit checks rather than assumes.

\paragraph{Blocked, not silently dropped} SubgraphX (via DIG) and ShapeGGen (via
GraphXAI) could not be installed: both need the pre-2.5 PyG
\texttt{torch\_sparse}/\texttt{torch\_scatter} compiled extensions, for which no
wheel exists at the pinned torch version. BA-2Motifs' download returns HTTP 403
here. All three are logged as blocked, occupy no row in any table, and are
excluded from every aggregate. ShapeGGen's exact-ground-truth role is covered by
the offline SynthMotifs generator; SubgraphX's absence is a genuine gap in
attributor coverage.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the audit buys} The single most useful thing MolSanity produces
is not a score but a \emph{negative certificate}: for \nNoGtCells{} of the
\nCellsDone{} committed cells --- every real molecular dataset in the sweep ---
attribution correctness cannot be measured at all, because no per-atom
explanation labels exist. Those cells can be audited for faithfulness,
coherence, stability and calibration, and this paper's central result is that
none of those, individually or together, is a reliable stand-in for correctness
under shift. Making that gap explicit is more useful than filling it with a
metric that looks like correctness and is not.

\paragraph{How attributions should be reported} Three practices follow directly
from the matrix. (i) Report the regime. An attribution audited in-distribution
carries no warrant out of distribution, and the same attributor moves from best
to worst across regimes ($\rho=\attrRankRho{}$). (ii) Report the backbone. The
backbone ordering does not transfer between datasets
($\rho=\backboneRankRho{}$), so ``IG on a GNN'' is under-specified. (iii) Report
the predictive metrics next to the explanation metrics, because a faithful
explanation of an anti-predictive model is exactly what the
\worstAucDataset{}~×~\worstAucBackbone{} cell shows you can get.

\paragraph{Why reliability is regime-, backbone- and dataset-dependent} A
faithfulness metric asks about the model; a ground-truth metric asks about the
chemistry; they agree only when the model has learned the chemistry. That
coincidence is engineered into synthetic benchmarks and is not guaranteed on
molecular data, particularly out of distribution. Attribution evaluation
inherits the model's inductive limitations, which is why the correct object of
study is the cell --- (dataset, backbone, attributor, split) --- and not the
attributor.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Incomplete grid} \nCellsPlanDone{} of \nCellsPlanned{} planned
cell-runs are complete. \nPendingRandom{} of the \nCellsPending{} pending runs
are the random-split in-distribution arm, which is exactly what is needed to
turn the fidelity-only ranking experiment into a within-dataset contrast. Until
those land, regime and dataset remain confounded in the headline experiment,
and the shift claim rests on a synthetic-versus-molecular comparison plus the
within-MUTAG faithful-but-wrong result.

\paragraph{Reduced budget} No committed cell was run at the publication budget.
Per-cell molecule counts are 20--30 (120 for the powered arm), so per-cell
confidence intervals are wide and most paired contrasts on the shift cells are
underpowered (Table~\ref{tab:paired}). Absolute values should be expected to
move when the full run completes; what we would expect to survive is the
qualitative dissociation, and that expectation is itself a claim this draft
cannot yet verify.

\paragraph{Proxy ground truth} MUTAG's mask is a nitro/aromatic-nitro SMARTS
proxy, not annotator labels. It is chemically motivated~\cite{mutag} but it
encodes an assumption about which substructure ought to matter, and Faber et
al.~\cite{faber} give the general reason that assumption can be wrong: the
trained model may use a different, equally valid rationale. Where the
distinction changes the reading, exact ground truth comes from the synthetic
sets.

\paragraph{Synthetic ground truth is not molecular} SynthMotifs and
SynthMotifsXL have exact node labels but are structural, non-molecular graphs.
They validate the audit machinery and support the in-distribution arm; they do
not establish chemical generalisation.

\paragraph{Single split, single seed} Each cell is one deterministic split at
one seed. Cross-checkpoint stability partially probes run-to-run variability,
but it is not a substitute for repeated splits, and no confidence interval in
this paper covers split variance.

\paragraph{Occlusion as a counterfactual} Zeroing node features takes the graph
off the data manifold; a motif whose removal barely moves the output may be
redundant rather than unimportant. This is a known weakness of
occlusion/fidelity-style metrics generally, and it bears most heavily on the
regression cells, where we decline to interpret the sign.

\paragraph{Coverage of attributors} SubgraphX is absent for dependency reasons,
and PGExplainer is absent from regression by construction. Conclusions about
the perturbation family rest on GNNExplainer and PGExplainer alone.

\paragraph{Multiplicity} $p$-values are unadjusted across a large number of
paired comparisons; individual contrasts in Table~\ref{tab:paired} should be
read as descriptive.

\section{Conclusion}

Molecular GNN attributions are audited today for whether they describe the
model. Across \nCellsDone{} committed audit cells we find that this is not the
same question as whether they describe the chemistry, and that the two questions
diverge in the regime molecular property prediction actually operates in. In
\nFaithfulButWrong{} of \nGtOccCells{} ground-truth cells an attribution is
positively faithful and anti-aligned with the true motif; ranking attributors by
field-standard fidelity recovers the ground-truth-best method in distribution
and fails under scaffold shift; and the ranking of both attributors and
backbones is regime-specific rather than universal. MolSanity's contribution is
the audit that makes those failures visible --- and, for the majority of real
molecular cells, the explicit finding that correctness is not measurable at all
with the labels the field currently has. The grid is still filling in; the
pipeline, the figures and every number here regenerate from the committed
artifacts as it does.
