\section{Introduction}

A medicinal chemist is shown a graph neural network's prediction that a
candidate is hERG-active, together with a heat map over its atoms. The heat map
concentrates on a piperidine nitrogen; the chemist finds that plausible, and the
series is deprioritised. Nothing in that workflow ever established that the
highlighted atoms are the atoms the model actually used, that they are the atoms
that matter chemically, or that the highlighting behaves the same way on the
next scaffold. Each of those is a separate question, and the answers come apart.

They come apart in a way that is measurable, and that gets worse in exactly the
setting drug discovery operates in. An attribution can be \emph{faithful}, in
that the atoms it ranks highest really are the atoms whose removal moves the
model's output most, and simultaneously \emph{wrong}, in that those atoms are
not the substructure that determines the property. Across the \nGtCells{}
committed cells in this study that carry a node-level ground truth,
\nFaithfulButWrong{} are positively faithful to the model and yet
\emph{anti}-aligned with the true motif (ground-truth AUROC below the chance
value of $0.5$). A benchmark that scores only faithfulness rates all of them as
fine.

In practice the problem takes the form of a selection problem. With no ground
truth available, which is the situation on every real molecular dataset in this
study, an attributor has to be chosen using a faithfulness or fidelity metric.
On the two cell families that do have a ground truth and were audited on both
splits, we can check whether that choice is the right one while changing
\emph{only} the split. Under a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split on MUTAG, all
$\mutShiftNMetrics{}$ ranking metrics (our occlusion measure, Fidelity+ and the
GraphFramEx characterisation score alike) select \mutShiftPickOccspearman{},
whose agreement with the mutagenic nitro motif is GT AUROC
$\mutShiftPickGtOccspearman{}$, while the ground truth ranks \mutShiftGtBest{}
best at $\mutShiftGtBestVal{}$. The median per-molecule gap is
$\mutShiftGapOccspearman{}$ AUROC (paired Wilcoxon
$p\mutShiftPOccspearman{}$, $n=\mutShiftNPairedOccspearman{}$; Benjamini--Hochberg
$q\mutShiftQOccspearman{}$ across the $\nSelectionTests{}$ selection tests), and
the rank correlation between faithfulness and correctness is \emph{negative}
($\mutShiftRhoOccspearman{}$ to $\mutShiftRhoCharacterization{}$). In
distribution on the same dataset, two of the three metrics instead select the
ground-truth-best attributor and the rank correlation is
$+\mutIndRhoOccspearman{}$.

None of this makes faithfulness metrics wrong. They answer a different question
from the one a chemist is asking, and the gap between the two questions widens
under distribution shift. Molecular property prediction is deployed under
scaffold shift almost by construction, since the point of a screening model is
to score series the training set does not contain, and scaffold-split
evaluation is now standard practice for the \emph{predictions}
themselves~\cite{mjepa,moleculenet}. The evaluation protocol for the
\emph{explanations} has not followed: it does not stratify by that shift at all.

\paragraph{Contributions} This paper presents \textbf{MolSanity}, a
reliability-audit framework for feature attributions on molecular GNNs. Rather
than propose another attribution method, it wraps the canonical
implementations (Captum~\cite{captum}, PyTorch Geometric~\cite{pyg},
RDKit~\cite{rdkit}) and audits them. Specifically:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item We define a six-axis audit over a motif-native (RDKit) decomposition:
  coherence, occlusion--attribution faithfulness, ground-truth localisation,
  cross-checkpoint stability, calibration linkage and confidence/correctness
  regime stratification, with field-standard Fidelity$\pm$, sparsity, the
  GraphFramEx characterisation score~\cite{graphframex} and the PyG/DIG
  unfaithfulness metric~\cite{dig} computed on the \emph{same} molecules for
  direct comparability (\nameref{sec:framework}).
\item We show that a faithfulness-only ranking picks the wrong attributor under
  scaffold shift, in a design where only the split varies, so the effect is not
  confounded with a change of dataset (\nameref{sec:selection}).
\item We separate what shift breaks from what it leaves alone. The
  \emph{backbone} ordering does not survive the change of split
  ($\rho=\bbRankRho{}$ over $\bbRankN{}$ backbones), so which architecture
  localises best in distribution says little about which does under shift. The
  \emph{attributor} ordering largely does survive ($\rho=\mutAttrRankRho{}$ on
  MUTAG, $\molAttrRankRho{}$ on \molDataset{}), and the level of faithfulness
  does not shift systematically either (across the $\nSplitPairs{}$ cells
  audited on both splits the median change is $\medSplitOccDelta{}$). What
  breaks is neither of those but the \emph{relationship} between faithfulness
  and correctness --- which is precisely the quantity a faithfulness-only
  benchmark cannot see.
\item Using \nRecords{} per-molecule audit records, we
  stratify reliability by confidence/correctness regime and test the
  calibration-linkage hypothesis directly. It holds per cell
  (median $\rho=\medCalibRho{}$, $\nCalibSigPos{}$ cells significantly positive
  vs.\ $\nCalibSigNeg{}$ negative) but \emph{attenuates to
  $\pooledCalibRho{}$} when cells are pooled, a Simpson's-paradox trap for
  anyone reporting a single aggregate number.
\item We report the negatives with the positives: a regression faithfulness
  operator we mis-specified, diagnosed and corrected, ground-truth arms
  whose scaffold split is degenerate and are therefore excluded from the shift
  contrast rather than counted toward it, an exactly-labelled molecular arm too
  saturated to adjudicate the central claim, and the \nNoGtCells{} cells where
  the correctness axis simply does not exist and is marked as such rather than
  scored.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Attribution methods} The attributors audited here are the standard
ones. The gradient family (Saliency~\cite{saliency}, Input×Gradient, Guided
Backpropagation~\cite{guidedbp} and Integrated Gradients~\cite{ig}) is
model-agnostic and cheap. Graph-specific methods learn a mask over the
input: GNNExplainer~\cite{gnnexplainer} optimises a soft edge/feature mask per
instance, PGExplainer~\cite{pgexplainer} amortises that into a parametric mask
network, and SubgraphX~\cite{subgraphx} searches connected subgraphs with
Monte-Carlo tree search under a Shapley objective. Graph analogues of
CAM/Grad-CAM were introduced by Pope et al.~\cite{gradcamgraph}; Yuan et
al.~\cite{gnnsurvey} survey the space. In every case we call the published
implementation rather than reimplementing it.

\paragraph{Evaluating explanations} Two evaluation traditions exist.
\emph{Faithfulness} measures whether the explanation tracks the model:
Fidelity$\pm$ and sparsity~\cite{gnnsurvey}, the GraphFramEx characterisation
score that combines them~\cite{graphframex}, and the unfaithfulness metric
shipped with DIG/PyG~\cite{dig}. \emph{Correctness} measures whether the
explanation recovers a known rationale, which requires ground-truth
substructures, typically synthetic~\cite{graphxai}. The tension between the
two is known: Faber et al.~\cite{faber} argue that comparing against ground
truth is misleading when the trained model does not use that rationale, and
Sanchez-Lengeling et al.~\cite{sanchezlengeling} built the first systematic
attribution benchmark for molecular GNNs on exactly this basis. Outside graphs,
sanity checks~\cite{sanitychecks} and retraining-based benchmarks~\cite{roar}
made a parallel case that plausible-looking attributions can be uninformative.
MolSanity's position is that neither axis subsumes the other, and that what
needs measuring is the \emph{joint} behaviour of the two under distribution
shift.

\paragraph{Frameworks, and the explicit delta} Four lines of work are close
enough that a citation is not a sufficient answer. Table~\ref{tab:related}
scores all of them on the same capabilities; what follows states what each row
of that table means.

\emph{Sanchez-Lengeling et al.}~\cite{sanchezlengeling} is the nearest
neighbour and the earliest. It built the first systematic attribution benchmark
for molecular GNNs on tasks whose ground-truth substructure is known by
construction, establishing that attribution methods should be scored against a
known rationale rather than against how plausible a chemist finds the
highlighting. MolSanity inherits that premise. What it adds is the faithfulness
axis measured on the \emph{same} molecules and the joint behaviour of the two.
A benchmark that reports correctness alone cannot show that a faithfulness-based
choice of attributor is the wrong choice, because it never makes that choice.
The selection-consistency test of \nameref{sec:selectiondef} makes it, and its
outcome changes with the split.

\emph{MolFaith}~\cite{molfaith} is the closest work on the faithfulness axis
and is larger than this study on that axis: eight attribution methods, two
molecular representations and five architectures over roughly $14{,}000$
molecules, against the $\nRecords{}$ per-molecule records here. We make no
claim to broader faithfulness coverage. Two of its findings bear directly on
ours. First, it reports that some methods are inherently more faithful than
others largely independently of architecture. We find the same stability from
the other direction: faithfulness does not shift systematically across the
$\nSplitPairs{}$ cells audited on both splits (median change
$\medSplitOccDelta{}$). Two independent studies at different scales agreeing
that faithfulness is a comparatively stable property of the method is the
premise our result then acts on. The correctness ordering is also fairly
stable across the split ($\rho=\mutAttrRankRho{}$ on MUTAG,
$\molAttrRankRho{}$ on \molDataset{}). Both rankings are stable; what changes
is how they sit relative to each other, and under shift they sit at odds. That
is the distinction we would press: \emph{stability of a proxy is not validity
of a proxy}. A practitioner with no rationale labels who selects a method by
faithfulness inherits a reproducible answer, not a correct one. Second,
MolFaith reports that faithfulness varies markedly per molecule; our regime
stratification locates part of that variation, since ground-truth localisation
degrades precisely on confidently-wrong predictions while faithfulness on those
same molecules \emph{improves}.

\emph{GraphXAI}~\cite{graphxai} and \emph{GraphFramEx}~\cite{graphframex} are
general-graph frameworks: GraphXAI supplies generators with exact node ground
truth and an evaluation library, GraphFramEx systematises fidelity-style
evaluation across explainers and tasks. Both are broader than MolSanity across
graph domains and narrower within chemistry. MolSanity scores chemically
meaningful units (SSSR rings, Murcko scaffold, BRICS fragments) rather than
individual atoms, and stratifies by the split that governs molecular
deployment. \emph{DIG}~\cite{dig} is a library rather than a protocol; we wrap
its unfaithfulness metric, and its SubgraphX implementation is the coverage gap
recorded in \nameref{sec:limits}.

\emph{What is new here, and what is not.} Ground-truth localisation,
fidelity metrics, motif decompositions and scaffold splits all exist already.
Two rows of Table~\ref{tab:related} we do not find in any of the four:
cross-checkpoint stability and calibration linkage. The contribution we would
defend is neither of those rows alone, but the result they were built to
support: that the two evaluation traditions above come apart under the shift
molecular models are deployed into --- far enough that the faithfulness
ranking and the correctness ranking reverse relative to one another --- and
that no metric on the faithfulness side signals it.

\section{The MolSanity audit}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Notation}

A molecule is a graph $G=(V,E)$ with node features $X\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|\times d}$
and edge features $Z\in\mathbb{R}^{|E|\times d'}$. A trained model
$f_\theta$ maps $G$ to logits $f_\theta(G)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ (classification) or
a scalar (regression); $t$ indexes the explained output. An \emph{attributor}
is a map $A:(f_\theta,G,t)\mapsto a\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ assigning a real score
to every atom.

RDKit decomposes $G$ into a motif cover
$\mathcal{M}(G)=\{M_1,\dots,M_K\}$, $M_k\subseteq V$, from SSSR rings, the
Bemis--Murcko scaffold~\cite{murcko} and BRICS-style fragments. Writing
$a^+_v=\max(a_v,0)$, the motif-level score is
\begin{equation}
  s_k \;=\; \sum_{v\in M_k} a^+_v ,
  \qquad k=1,\dots,K .
  \label{eq:motifscore}
\end{equation}
Working at motif rather than atom granularity is what makes the audit
chemically legible: a chemist reasons about a nitro group, not about atom 14.

For $S\subseteq V$ let $G\!\setminus\!S$ denote $G$ with the features of $S$
zeroed through the model's node-mask multiplier,
$X' = X\odot(\mathbf{1}-\mathbb{1}_S)$, leaving the topology intact.

\subsection{The five audit statistics}

\paragraph{(i) Faithfulness} Occlusion of motif $M_k$ changes the explained
output by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_k \;=\; f_{\theta,t}(G)\;-\;f_{\theta,t}(G\!\setminus\! M_k).
  \label{eq:occ}
\end{equation}
An attribution is faithful when it ranks motifs as occlusion does, measured by
the Spearman rank correlation over the $K$ motifs of one molecule,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\mathrm{occ}}(G) \;=\; \operatorname{Spearman}\!\big(
    (s_k)_{k\le K},\,(\Delta_k)_{k\le K}\big) \in[-1,1].
  \label{eq:rhoocc}
\end{equation}
$\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}=1$ means the attributor recovers the causal ordering
exactly; $\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}=0$ means it is uninformative about it; and
$\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}<0$ means it \emph{inverts} it, which is strictly worse
than an uninformative explanation. Undefined cases ($K<2$, or zero variance in
either argument) are recorded as missing, never as $0$.

We also reproduce the field-standard fidelity pair~\cite{gnnsurvey}. With
$\tau$ the $80$th percentile of $a^+$ and $S_\tau=\{v: a^+_v\ge\tau\}$ the
salient set, and $p_t(\cdot)$ the softmax probability of the explained class,
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Fid}^{+} &= p_t(G) - p_t(G\!\setminus\!S_\tau), \label{eq:fidplus}\\
  \mathrm{Fid}^{-} &= p_t(G) - p_t(G\!\setminus\!\bar{S_\tau}), \label{eq:fidminus}
\end{align}
higher $\mathrm{Fid}^{+}$ and lower $\mathrm{Fid}^{-}$ being better, with
sparsity $1-|S_\tau|/|V|$. The GraphFramEx characterisation
score~\cite{graphframex} combines them as the weighted harmonic mean
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{char} \;=\;
  \frac{(w_{+}+w_{-})\,\mathrm{Fid}^{+}\,(1-\mathrm{Fid}^{-})}
       {w_{-}\mathrm{Fid}^{+} + w_{+}(1-\mathrm{Fid}^{-})},
  \qquad w_{+}=w_{-}=\tfrac12 ,
  \label{eq:char}
\end{equation}
undefined outside $\mathrm{Fid}^{\pm}\in[0,1]$. That constraint matters for
the regression cells, where $\Delta$ is taken in output space and
$\mathrm{Fid}^{\pm}$ leaves that range.

\paragraph{(ii) Correctness} Where a node-level ground-truth mask
$g\in\{0,1\}^{|V|}$ exists, correctness is the ability of $a$ to separate the
true substructure from the rest, scored by the area under the ROC curve of the
min--max normalised attribution against $g$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{GT\text{-}AUROC}(G) =\;
      &\Pr\big[\tilde a_u > \tilde a_v \,\big|\, g_u{=}1,\, g_v{=}0\big]\\[-1pt]
      &+\tfrac12\Pr\big[\tilde a_u = \tilde a_v\big],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:gtauroc}
\end{equation}
with $u,v$ drawn uniformly from the positive and negative atoms. Chance is
$\tfrac12$; values below $\tfrac12$ mean the attribution is \emph{anti-aligned},
ranking the true motif systematically \emph{below} the rest. When $g$ is
degenerate ($\sum_v g_v\in\{0,|V|\}$) the quantity is undefined and reported as
such.

\paragraph{(iii) Coherence} Concentration and connectedness of the attribution
mass: the Gini coefficient of $a^+$, the fraction of mass in the top $20\%$ of
atoms, the largest-connected-component fraction of $S_\tau$ in the induced
subgraph, and the motif top-1 share $\max_k s_k/\sum_k s_k$. These are
descriptive; \nameref{sec:crosstask} shows they carry little reliability
signal.

\paragraph{(iv) Stability} With $\theta^{\mathrm{early}}$ an intermediate
checkpoint of the same training run and $s^{\mathrm{early}}$, $s^{\mathrm{final}}$
the corresponding motif scores~\eqref{eq:motifscore},
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{stab}(G) \;=\;
  \operatorname{Spearman}\big(s^{\mathrm{early}},\,s^{\mathrm{final}}\big).
  \label{eq:stab}
\end{equation}
An explanation that reorders motifs between two checkpoints of one run is a
weak basis for a chemistry decision.

\paragraph{(v) Regime and calibration} Logits are temperature-scaled on the
validation fold~\cite{calibration}; let $c(G)=\max_j p_j(G)$ be the calibrated
confidence and $y(G)\in\{0,1\}$ the correctness of the prediction. With
$\tau_c=0.8$, each molecule is assigned
\begin{equation}
  R(G)=
  \begin{cases}
    \textsf{conf-correct} & c\ge\tau_c,\; y=1,\\
    \textsf{conf-error}   & c\ge\tau_c,\; y=0,\\
    \textsf{borderline}   & c<\tau_c .
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:regime}
\end{equation}
Calibration linkage is the within-cell rank correlation between per-molecule
calibration quality and reliability,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\mathrm{cal}} \;=\; \operatorname{Spearman}\big(1-|c-y|,\;
  \rho_{\mathrm{occ}}\big),
  \label{eq:callink}
\end{equation}
positive values supporting the hypothesis that better-calibrated predictions
carry more trustworthy attributions.

\subsection{The selection-consistency test}
\label{sec:selectiondef}

The audit's central question is operational rather than descriptive. A
practitioner with no ground truth must choose an attributor using a
faithfulness statistic; we ask whether that choice agrees with the one the
ground truth would make.

Fix a \emph{cell} $c=(\mathcal{D},f_\theta,\pi)$, meaning a dataset, a trained
backbone and a split, together with a set $\mathcal{A}$ of attributors, each audited on
the same molecule sample $\mathcal{G}_c$. For attributor $A$ write
$\bar m(A)=|\mathcal{G}_c|^{-1}\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_c} m_A(G)$ for the cell
mean of statistic $m$. Define the ground-truth-optimal and the
faithfulness-optimal attributor
\begin{equation}
  A^{\star} = \arg\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}} \overline{\mathrm{GT}}(A),
  \qquad
  \hat A_m = \arg\max_{A\in\mathcal{A}} \bar m(A),
  \label{eq:argmax}
\end{equation}
for each faithfulness statistic $m\in\{\rho_{\mathrm{occ}},\mathrm{Fid}^{+},
\mathrm{char}\}$. Two quantities follow. The \emph{selection gap} is the paired
per-molecule difference in correctness between the two choices,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_m(G) = \mathrm{GT}_{A^{\star}}(G)-\mathrm{GT}_{\hat A_m}(G),
  \qquad G\in\mathcal{G}_c ,
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
tested against $H_0:\operatorname{median}\delta_m=0$ with a two-sided Wilcoxon
signed-rank test on the molecules both attributors audited. The
\emph{selection-consistency coefficient} is the rank correlation between the
two orderings over $\mathcal{A}$,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{m}^{\mathrm{sel}} \;=\;
  \operatorname{Spearman}_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\!\big(\bar m(A),\,
  \overline{\mathrm{GT}}(A)\big) \in[-1,1].
  \label{eq:selrho}
\end{equation}
$\rho^{\mathrm{sel}}\!\approx\!1$ means faithfulness is a sound surrogate for
correctness in that cell; $\rho^{\mathrm{sel}}\!\approx\!0$ means it is
uninformative; $\rho^{\mathrm{sel}}<0$ means it is actively misleading, in that
ranking by faithfulness does worse than ranking at random. Because
$\mathcal{D}$, $f_\theta$ and $\mathcal{A}$ are held fixed and only $\pi$
varies between the two arms of each comparison, any change in
$\rho^{\mathrm{sel}}$ is attributable to the split rather than to a change of
dataset or model.

\subsection{Estimation and inference}

Cell means are reported with $95\%$ percentile bootstrap intervals over
molecules ($2000$ resamples, fixed seed). Attributor comparisons within a cell
are paired on molecule identity and tested with the two-sided Wilcoxon
signed-rank test; correlations are Spearman throughout, as all statistics here
are ordinal in nature and several are heavy-tailed. Sample sizes are the
audited molecules per cell (20--120), which is small enough that we treat
individual $p$-values as descriptive and report them unadjusted for
multiplicity, leaning on replication across cells rather than on any single
test. Where a statistic is undefined for a molecule it is dropped from that
statistic's mean only, and the retained $n$ is reported alongside.

\subsection{Implementation}

The pipeline is staged and idempotent: each stage writes a marker keyed to a
content hash of its configuration, every cell is isolated so one failure cannot
abort a sweep, and trained checkpoints are content-addressed so an audit
reproduces without retraining. Seeds, library versions, git revision and
hardware are recorded per run. Attributors are the canonical
implementations~\cite{captum,pyg}, wrapped behind one interface rather than
reimplemented.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets} Tier-1 provides the ground-truth anchor. Two arms are
molecular with ground truth that is \emph{exact by construction}:
\textbf{MolMotif} and \textbf{MolMotifHard} take real drug-like molecules
(BBBP and Tox21 respectively) and relabel them by the presence of a chemical
substructure --- an aromatic halogen and a carboxylic acid --- so the
substructure \emph{defines} the label and its atoms are the node ground truth
by definition rather than by assumption. MolMotifHard exists because MolMotif's
halogen is a rare element the model can detect atom-wise, saturating the task;
a carboxylic acid is built only from carbon and oxygen, so the model must learn
the arrangement (\S\ref{sec:selection}). \textbf{MUTAG}~\cite{mutag,tudataset}
is molecular with the nitro proxy. Three arms have exact node labels but are
not molecules: \textbf{SynthMotifs} and its larger twin
\textbf{SynthMotifsXL}, generated offline (Barab\'asi--Albert base plus
house/cycle motifs); \textbf{BA-2Motifs}~\cite{pgexplainer}; and
\textbf{ShapeGGen}~\cite{graphxai}, a node-classification benchmark entered
here at the graph level by taking each labelled node's $k$-hop enclosing
subgraph, with $k$ the message-passing depth. Tier-2 is
MoleculeNet~\cite{moleculenet}: \textbf{BBBP} and \textbf{BACE}
(classification), \textbf{ESOL}, \textbf{FreeSolv} and \textbf{Lipophilicity}
(regression). Tier-3 covers chemistry-meets-medicine endpoints:
\textbf{ClinTox}, \textbf{SIDER} and \textbf{Tox21} as single-task binary views
of the MoleculeNet panels, and \textbf{DILI} (drug-induced liver injury) and
\textbf{hERG} (cardiotoxicity) via the Therapeutics Data Commons~\cite{tdc}.
TDC sets are featurised with the same PyG encoding as MoleculeNet so the
backbones are drop-in. Credential-gated resources are out of scope and never
worked around.

\paragraph{Backbones} GINE~\cite{gin,pretraingnn}, GCN~\cite{gcn},
GAT~\cite{gat}, MPNN~\cite{mpnn} and AttentiveFP~\cite{attentivefp}, sharing a
pooling head, calibration and splits.

\paragraph{Attributors} Integrated Gradients, Saliency, Guided Backpropagation
and Input×Gradient through Captum~\cite{captum}; GNNExplainer and PGExplainer
through PyTorch Geometric~\cite{pyg}; SubgraphX~\cite{subgraphx} through
DIG~\cite{dig}. PGExplainer is classification-only by construction: its mask
network trains against class logits, so it is absent from the regression
cells. SubgraphX is scheduled on the ground-truth arms only --- the cells where
correctness can be measured --- because its Monte-Carlo tree search costs
roughly $\cellCostRatio{}\times$ an Integrated Gradients cell-run
(\S\ref{sec:repro}); on those arms it is a full column at the same sample size
as every other attributor, not a reduced-budget approximation.

\paragraph{Splits} The primary split is a deterministic, class-aware
Bemis--Murcko scaffold split~\cite{murcko}, the protocol used to evaluate
molecular property models under realistic generalisation~\cite{mjepa} (whole scaffold groups move together,
so there is no leakage, but every fold is guaranteed to contain every class),
with a random split as the in-distribution reference. Imbalanced classification
uses inverse-frequency class weighting and AUC-based model selection. Every
comparison in this paper that is labelled ``shift'' versus ``in-distribution''
is these two splits on the same dataset.

\paragraph{Scale of the study} The sweep trains every (dataset, backbone)
pair on both splits and audits every (dataset, backbone, attributor, split)
combination, giving \nDone{} audited combinations and \nRecords{} per-molecule
audit records in total. Audited samples are 20--120 molecules per combination,
drawn from the held-out test fold. Exact software versions, hardware, seed and
the per-combination outcome ledger are recorded with the released artifacts
(\nameref{sec:availability}).

If faithfulness and correctness dissociate, then ranking attributors by a
faithfulness metric, which is what an evaluation framework must do when no

$\mutShiftGapOccspearman{}$ with $p\mutShiftPOccspearman{}$, and
$q\mutShiftQOccspearman{}$ after controlling the false discovery rate over all
$\nSelectionTests{}$ selection tests, so this contrast is not an artefact of
testing many of them. The

$\mutShiftRhoOccspearman{}$ for occlusion $\rho$,
$\mutShiftRhoFidelityplus{}$ for Fidelity+ and
$\mutShiftRhoCharacterization{}$ for characterisation. A framework ranking on
faithfulness here fails to find the best attributor and systematically
prefers the worst.

\paragraph{MUTAG, in distribution} The same experiment on the random split
behaves differently. Occlusion $\rho$ and Fidelity+ both select
\mutIndPickOccspearman{}, which is also the ground-truth-best, and their rank
correlation with truth is $+\mutIndRhoOccspearman{}$. The characterisation

$\mutIndPickGtCharacterization{}$

with rank

only much less dangerous.

over all $\nPooledCells{}$ cells of the $\nPooledArms{}$ molecular

$+\pooledIndRho{}$ in distribution ($p=\pooledIndP{}$) to $\pooledShiftRho{}$

($\pooledIndFaith{}$ to $\pooledShiftFaith{}$, $p=\pooledShiftFaithP{}$) and
ground-truth localisation does not move ($p=\pooledShiftGtP{}$).

attributor is correct, which argues for measuring correctness rather than for
building a better proxy.

\subsection{Does the model actually read the rationale?}
\label{sec:rationale}

\ifHasFaberPartition
The standing objection to every result above is Faber et al.'s~\cite{faber}:
scoring an attribution against a labelled substructure is misleading if the
trained model does not use that substructure, in which case a low GT AUROC is a
fact about the model rather than about the explanation. The objection is
correct, and it is testable rather than merely arguable. For each molecule we
occlude the ground-truth substructure and record whether the prediction
collapses; if it does, the model demonstrably reads that substructure, and its
attribution can be judged against it on the model's own behaviour.

The objection applies to more molecules than it does not.
\nFaberIgnores{} of the audited molecules carry a rationale the model does not
rely on, against \nFaberUses{} that it does, and the split is visible in the
scores: mean GT AUROC is $\faberGtUses{}$ where the model reads the rationale
and $\faberGtIgnores{}$ where it does not. A framework that ignored this
distinction would be scoring the model under the guise of scoring the
explanation, on roughly three molecules in five.

The number that settles it is what remains. Among the \nFaberUses{} molecules
whose prediction demonstrably depends on the labelled substructure,
\nFaberAntiDespiteUse{} --- a fraction of $\faberFracAntiDespiteUse{}$ ---
receive an attribution \emph{anti-aligned} with it: the substructure the model
provably uses is ranked among the atoms the attribution deems least relevant.
On those molecules no appeal to an alternative rationale is available. The
attribution misdescribes a model by that model's own behaviour, and
faithfulness metrics computed on the same molecules do not flag it. We
therefore treat the Faber objection as a partition to report rather than a
defeater: it removes a majority of molecules from the correctness argument, and
what survives it is still a large, unambiguous population of wrong
explanations.
\else
This run produced no molecules with both a rationale-reliance measurement and a
ground-truth score, so the Faber partition is not computable here and no claim
is made about it.
\fi

\subsection{What to do when there is no ground truth}
\label{sec:abstention}

\ifHasAbstention
Every result above depends on rationale labels, which is exactly what a working
medicinal chemist does not have. If the audit only says ``your attributions may
be wrong and you cannot check'', it has diagnosed a problem and left the reader
where it found them. The weaker but usable question is not \emph{which
attributor should I trust} but \emph{on which molecules should I decline to
trust this one} --- a selective-prediction framing, transplanted from
prediction to explanation. We rank $\nAbstainSignals{}$ candidate signals, all
computable at inference time without any labels, by the gain in mean
ground-truth localisation between full coverage and half coverage
(Figure~\ref{fig:abstention}).

The best is \abstainBest{} ($+\abstainBestLift{}$ over
$\nAbstainRecords{}$ molecules with ground truth). Ranking by it and keeping
the most confident \abstainCoverage{}
\ifHasAbstainOp
cuts the share of retained molecules whose attribution is \emph{below chance}
from $\abstainFullBelow{}$ to $\abstainBelow{}$, with mean localisation
$\abstainKeptGt{}$ over the $\nAbstainKept{}$ retained
($n=\nAbstainKept{}$).
\fi
We state the rule against the below-chance share rather than the mean
deliberately: pooled mean localisation is already tolerable at full coverage,
so a rule stated against it would look successful while leaving the failures
untouched. The tail is what a deployment gets hurt by, and the tail is what
this moves.

The ranking's negative result is as useful as its positive one.
$\nAbstainNeg{}$ of the $\nAbstainSignals{}$ signals has \emph{negative} lift:
\abstainWorst{} ($\abstainWorstLift{}$). Abstaining on molecules whose
attribution mass is spread across many motifs --- keeping those that
concentrate on a single chemically coherent one --- makes the retained set
\emph{worse}. That is the intuition a chemist is most likely to apply by eye,
and on this data it is anti-predictive. An audit that reported only the working
signal would have left that trap in place.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/fig_abstention.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Coverage--reliability curves for abstention signals, over the

\paragraph{What this rule does not license} These curves are fitted where
ground truth exists. Applying them to a dataset where it does not assumes the
signal--reliability relationship transfers, and the central finding of this
paper is that one such relationship --- faithfulness to correctness --- does
not survive a change of split. We therefore offer the rule as a calibrated
starting point that a group can re-fit on its own labelled subset, not as a
constant of nature. Stating that constraint is not hedging: it is the same
standard we hold the faithfulness metrics to.
\else
This run produced no records carrying both an abstention signal and a
ground-truth score, so the coverage--reliability analysis is not computable
here and no rule is proposed.
\fi

correct group at or above the most correct one. The confident-error estimate
rests on $\nRegCEGt{}$ molecules and is reported with that $n$ throughout; it
is suggestive rather than precise.

better-calibrated predictions carry more trustworthy attributions. Computed per

against $\nCalibSigNeg{}$ significantly negative), but the effect is
cell-specific rather than universal. Pooling every molecule into a single
correlation instead yields $\pooledCalibRho{}$, roughly a quarter of the
per-cell median and indistinguishable from no relationship at all. The pooled
number is an artefact of mixing cells with different confidence distributions,
and we report it only to warn against it: an evaluation that reports one
aggregate correlation would conclude the linkage does not exist, when per cell
it does.

\subsection{Cross-task reliability}
\label{sec:crosstask}

Tables~\ref{tab:regression}, \ref{tab:molecular} and~\ref{tab:molecularb} give
every audited combination on a real molecular dataset. Three cross-task
patterns hold, and one apparent pattern turns out to be an artefact.

\paragraph{A mis-specified regression operator, found and corrected} An
earlier sweep reported negative occlusion faithfulness on most of the
\nRegCells{} regression combinations, on cells whose predictive performance was
well behaved ($R^2$ from $\esolRtwoMin{}$ to $\esolRtwoMax{}$ on ESOL). We did
not interpret that pattern, and on inspection it was an artefact of our own
operator rather than a property of the attributions. The operator compared two
different quantities: the attribution was clipped at zero, keeping only atoms
that push the prediction \emph{up}, while the occlusion effect kept its sign. A
motif driving a solubility prediction strongly down therefore scored as
unimportant on the attribution side and dominant on the occlusion side, which
is sufficient on its own to produce a systematic negative correlation.

The corrected operator ranks by magnitude on both sides, reports the
GraphFramEx characterisation score as undefined rather than clipping a
standard-deviation-space shift into $[0,1]$, and adds a bounded statistic
giving the share of the total occlusion effect carried by the salient atoms.
The sweep reported here uses it, and the pathology is gone: $\nRegNeg{}$ of the
$\nRegCells{}$ regression cells now show negative occlusion faithfulness, with
median $\medRegOcc{}$. We report the original error alongside the correction
because a systematic negative result that turns out to be an artefact of the
measuring instrument is the failure mode this whole paper is about, and we were
not exempt from it.

\paragraph{Coherence is high nearly everywhere, and says little} Motif top-1
share lies between $\motifTopMin{}$ and $\motifTopMax{}$ across the
\nMolecularCells{} molecular cells, with \nMotifInBand{} of them inside a
narrow $0.6$--$0.9$ band, largely independent of whether the same cell is
faithful or anti-faithful. Concentration on a single motif is easy to achieve
and, on this evidence, close to uninformative about reliability. It belongs in
the audit as a descriptive statistic, not as a quality score.

\paragraph{Stability is high but not uniform} Median cross-checkpoint stability
over \nStabilityCells{} cells is $\medStability{}$, with a minimum of
$\minStability{}$, a cell whose early and final attributions are essentially
uncorrelated. By attributor, GNNExplainer is the most stable
(median $\stabGNNE{}$) and Integrated Gradients sits at $\stabIG{}$ over
$\nStabIG{}$ cells; the per-attributor counts are very unequal in this run, so
these medians are descriptive rather than a controlled comparison.

Table~\ref{tab:paired} gives the paired attributor contrasts behind
Section~\nameref{sec:selection}, computed from the per-molecule records.

\subsection{Negative and degenerate results}
\label{sec:negative}

We record what did not work alongside what did.

\paragraph{Ground truth that was not being read} BA-2Motifs is a synthetic
benchmark whose whole purpose is to carry exact node labels. It trains in this
run and produces $2\times100$ audited molecules, but both its cells sit in the
no-ground-truth block, because the loader found no node labels to read. The
cause was ours rather than the dataset's: PyG's \texttt{BA2MotifDataset}
exposes only node features, an edge index and a graph-level label, and our
extractor looked for a per-node field that is never populated. The labels are
recoverable from the released node ordering, in which the injected motif is
appended after the Barab\'asi--Albert base. That recovery is now implemented and
verified structurally, by requiring the induced subgraph on those nodes to be a
house or a five-cycle before the mask is used, and cross-checked against a
generator that produces the same construction with independent labels. It is
refused rather than guessed when the check fails, so a reordered loader
degrades to the behaviour reported here rather than inventing a mask. The
recovery is in the sweep reported here: BA-2Motifs carries node-level ground
truth throughout and appears in Table~\ref{tab:tier1} as a ground-truth arm. It
is not a molecular one, so it takes no part in the shift contrast for the same
reason SynthMotifs and ShapeGGen do not, but the correctness axis is now
measured on it rather than reported as missing.

\ifHasDegenerateCells
\paragraph{Degenerate ground truth} $\nDegenerateGtCells{}$ ground-truth cells
return the identical value $\degenVal{}$ on every audited molecule, including
\degenCell{} ($n=\degenN{}$). A cell with zero between-molecule variance
cannot support a confidence interval or a paired test, and one of the two is
the attributor the in-distribution MUTAG arm names as ground-truth-best. We
report those cells with the rest and mark what rests on them.
\fi

\paragraph{The best model has the worst attributions} The highest-AUC molecular
classification cell in the run is \bestAucDataset{}·\bestAucBackbone{} under the
scaffold split, at accuracy $\bestAucAcc{}$, ROC-AUC $\bestAucAuc{}$ and ECE
$\bestAucEce{}$, so accurate and well calibrated. Its attributions are the most
\emph{anti}-faithful of any molecular cell in the matrix
($\rho=\bestAucOcc{}$): Integrated Gradients ranks the motifs of these
molecules in close to the reverse of their causal order. Predictive quality,
calibration and explanation reliability are three different axes.

\paragraph{A weak model can be faithfully explained} The converse also appears.
The most faithful molecular cell is
\mostFaithfulDataset{}·\mostFaithfulBackbone{} ($\rho=\mostFaithfulOcc{}$) on a
model with accuracy $\mostFaithfulAcc{}$ and ROC-AUC $\mostFaithfulAuc{}$,
barely better than chance. Faithfulness certifies that the explanation
describes the model; it says nothing about whether the model is worth
describing. Any reliability report that omits the predictive metrics can be
satisfied by this cell.

\paragraph{Accuracy is the wrong headline on imbalanced sets}
\nHighAccRows{} committed rows report test accuracy $\geq0.95$, with ROC-AUC as
low as $\minHighAccAuc{}$.
\ifHasTox
On Tox21, accuracy runs $\toxAccMin{}$--$\toxAccMax{}$
against AUC $\toxAucMin{}$--$\toxAucMax{}$.
\fi
We report AUC alongside accuracy
throughout for this reason, and the lowest-AUC cell in the run
(\worstAucDataset{}·\worstAucBackbone{}, AUC $\worstAucAuc{}$ at accuracy
$\worstAucAcc{}$) is exactly the case where accuracy alone would mislead.

\paragraph{Blocked, re-examined, and now populated} An earlier run recorded
SubgraphX (via DIG) and ShapeGGen (via GraphXAI) as uninstallable, on the
grounds that they need the compiled
\texttt{torch\_\-sparse}/\texttt{torch\_\-scatter} extensions for which no wheel
exists at the pinned torch version. That diagnosis was wrong in both cases:
the extensions build from source, DIG then installs, and GraphXAI installs from
a source checkout rather than a release. Both are integrated in the sweep
reported here. SubgraphX contributes a full attributor column on every
ground-truth arm and ShapeGGen contributes its own cells, so neither is a gap
in the tables any more.

We keep the record of the wrong diagnosis because it cost real coverage for two
sweeps, and because a dependency declared impossible is rarely re-examined once
it is written down. The instructive part is that nothing about the environment
changed between the two judgements --- only how hard we looked.

Two limitations survive the fix rather than being resolved by it. SubgraphX is
two to three orders of magnitude more expensive per molecule than the gradient
family, which is a selection pressure on which attributors get benchmarked at
all and which we discuss in \S\ref{sec:limits}. ShapeGGen is a
node-classification benchmark on a single large graph, whereas every axis of
this audit is defined per molecule at the graph level; it enters the audit as a
graph-level task by construction, and its scaffold split is degenerate for the
same reason the other non-molecular arms' are.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the audit buys} The single most useful thing MolSanity produces
is not a score but a \emph{negative certificate}. For \nNoGtCells{} of the
\nRowsTotal{} committed rows, covering every real molecular dataset in the
sweep, attribution correctness cannot be measured at all, because no per-atom
explanation labels exist. Those cells can be audited for faithfulness,
coherence, stability and calibration, and this paper's central result is that
under shift none of those is a reliable stand-in for correctness. Making that
gap explicit is more useful than filling it with a metric that looks like
correctness and is not.

\paragraph{How attributions should be reported} Four practices follow directly
from the matrix. (i) Report the regime: an attributor selected in distribution
carries no warrant out of it, and on the one cell family where we can check,
the selection is significantly wrong under shift. (ii) Report the backbone: the
backbone ordering does not survive the change of split ($\rho=\bbRankRho{}$),
so ``IG on a GNN'' is under-specified. (iii) Report the predictive metrics next
to the explanation metrics, because the accurate, well-calibrated
\bestAucDataset{}·\bestAucBackbone{} cell has the most anti-faithful
attributions in the matrix. (iv) Do not pool: the calibration-linkage
correlation changes sign between the per-cell and the pooled analysis.

\paragraph{Why reliability is regime-, backbone- and dataset-dependent} A
faithfulness metric asks about the model; a ground-truth metric asks about the
chemistry; they agree only when the model has learned the chemistry. That
coincidence is engineered into synthetic benchmarks and is not guaranteed on
molecular data, particularly out of distribution, which is the situation Faber
et al.~\cite{faber} identify as making ground-truth comparison hazardous in
general. Attribution evaluation inherits the model's inductive limitations, so
the correct object of study is the cell, (dataset, backbone, attributor,
split), and not the attributor alone.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Ground truth is scarce, and one arm of it is a proxy} The two cell
families that carry the argument have $n=\mutShiftNMol{}$ audited molecules per
arm. MUTAG, the molecular one, uses a chemically motivated nitro/aromatic-nitro
SMARTS proxy rather than annotator labels: it encodes an assumption about which
substructure ought to matter, and the trained model may use a different,
equally valid rationale~\cite{faber}. We answer that objection empirically
rather than by assertion (\S\ref{sec:rationale}), but it remains the case that
the shift contrast rests on this one dataset, at $n=\mutShiftNMol{}$ audited
molecules per arm, with a proxy ground truth.

The arms that might have corroborated it cannot. SynthMotifs, BA-2Motifs and
ShapeGGen have exact node labels but are not molecules, so a Bemis--Murcko
scaffold is undefined for them: our splitter reports every graph in its own
scaffold bucket, which makes their ``scaffold'' split an arbitrary
deterministic partition rather than a chemical shift, and we exclude them from
the contrast rather than let a degenerate partition stand in for one.
\molDataset{} and \hardDataset{} are molecular \emph{and} exactly labelled,
by construction, since the label is presence of a substructure. Their
limitation is not that they are saturated outright but that their attributor
rankings are \emph{bimodal}: under shift $\molShiftNAboveNine{}$ of
$\mutShiftNAttr{}$ on \molDataset{} and $\hardShiftNAboveNine{}$ on
\hardDataset{} sit above GT AUROC $0.9$ within a few hundredths of one
another, so a $\mutShiftNAttr{}$-point rank correlation on either arm is
carried by a handful of comparisons among effectively tied methods. We built
\hardDataset{} to widen that range and it does, but not enough to make either
arm decisive alone. No public dataset is simultaneously molecular,
exactly labelled and \emph{graded} in difficulty; constructing one is the
clearest next step for this line of work, and until it exists the pooled
estimate across arms is a better instrument than any single one of them.

\ifHasDegenerateCells
\paragraph{Degenerate ground-truth cells} $\nDegenerateGtCells{}$ cells return
an identical GT AUROC on every audited molecule, including \degenCell{}
($\degenVal{}$, $n=\degenN{}$). Zero between-molecule variance means the
bootstrap interval is uninformative and any ``best attributor'' verdict resting
on such a cell is fragile; we flag them rather than drop them, and the shift
arms that carry the argument do not involve them.
\fi

\paragraph{A dataset that was not scored, and now is} For two sweeps
BA-2Motifs contributed faithfulness only, appearing in the no-ground-truth
block despite being a synthetic benchmark designed to carry exact labels,
because our extractor did not read the ordering those labels are encoded in.
The recovery is in this sweep and BA-2Motifs is scored on the correctness axis
throughout. We leave the episode in the record because the failure was silent:
a dataset whose entire purpose is exact ground truth sat in the
\emph{no}-ground-truth block for two runs without anything flagging the
contradiction.

\paragraph{Three seeds, one split rule} Each cell is run at
$\nPooledSeeds{}$ seeds and every figure quoted per cell is an across-seed
mean, with the spread reported alongside it. What is \emph{not} varied is the
split rule itself: each regime is one deterministic partition, so no interval
here covers the variance of choosing a different scaffold-split implementation
or ratio. Per-cell molecule counts are also modest where the dataset is
small --- $n=\mutShiftNMol{}$ on the MUTAG scaffold fold, which is the entire
held-out set rather than a sample from it --- so individual contrasts remain
underpowered even at $\nPooledSeeds{}$ seeds, which is why the central claim
is made on the pooled estimate rather than on any single cell.

\paragraph{Occlusion as a counterfactual} Zeroing node features takes the graph
off the data manifold; a motif whose removal barely moves the output may be
redundant rather than unimportant. It bears most visibly on the regression cells, whose
output-space Fidelity$\pm$ values are shifts in units of the training target's
standard deviation and so leave the $[-1,1]$ a probability-space fidelity
occupies; they are comparable within a column and not across tasks.

\paragraph{Proxy ground truth} MUTAG's mask is a nitro/aromatic-nitro SMARTS
proxy, not annotator labels. It encodes an assumption about which substructure
ought to matter, and the trained model may use a different, equally valid
rationale~\cite{faber}.

\paragraph{Coverage of attributors} The search family is represented by
SubgraphX on the ground-truth arms only (\nCellSgx{} cell-runs), because its
cost is roughly $\cellCostRatio{}\times$ a gradient attributor's
(\S\ref{sec:repro}). Conclusions that pool over the whole matrix therefore
weight the gradient family more heavily than a cost-blind design would, and any
claim about search-based attribution outside the ground-truth arms is
unsupported here. This is a real coverage limit, and it is the same selection
pressure --- expensive attributors get benchmarked less --- that the audit is
meant to make visible rather than inherit silently.

\paragraph{Multiplicity} The selection tests carry Benjamini--Hochberg
adjusted $q$ values over the family of $\nSelectionTests{}$ tests, and the
attributor contrasts carry them over the family within each cell block, so the
headline mismatches are false-discovery-rate controlled rather than descriptive.
What the adjustment cannot repair is power: at $n=\mutShiftNMol{}$ molecules per
arm a contrast that fails to reach significance is weak evidence of no
difference, not evidence of none.

\section{Conclusion}

Molecular GNN attributions are audited today for whether they describe the
model. Across the committed audit matrix we find that this is not the same
question as whether they describe the chemistry, and that the two diverge in
the regime molecular property prediction actually operates in. On the one cell
family where exact ground truth, several attributors and both splits coexist, a
faithfulness-only ranking is harmless in distribution and selects a
significantly worse attributor under scaffold shift. MolSanity's own
faithfulness metric is no exception. Reliability does not transfer across
backbones or across splits, and for the \nNoGtCells{} molecular rows in the
matrix, correctness cannot be measured at all with the labels the field
currently has. The audit is what makes those failures visible; the pipeline,
the figures and every number here regenerate from the committed artifacts as
the grid fills in.

\section{Reproducibility and compute}
\label{sec:repro}

\paragraph{What is pinned} Every result in this paper comes from one
invocation of \texttt{\runConfig{}} at git revision \texttt{\runGitRev{}},
seeds \runSeedList{}, on Python~\runPython{} with
torch~\runTorch{}, PyTorch Geometric~\runPyG{}, RDKit~\runRDKit{},
Captum~\runCaptum{}, NumPy~\runNumpy{} and scikit-learn~\runSklearn{}. The run
manifest records all of these, the seed, the platform string and the CUDA
device count (\runDevices{} device visible), and is committed alongside the
results. Seeds are set for Python, NumPy and torch before every stage; splits
are deterministic functions of the dataset and seed, not of iteration order.

\paragraph{Resumability and caching} Each cell-run writes a marker keyed to a
content hash of its own configuration --- cell, split, budget, model, training
schedule and seed --- so re-invoking the sweep re-executes exactly the cells
whose configuration changed and reuses the rest. The hash is also what makes a
correction auditable: when we found and fixed a defect in the scaffold split
(\S\ref{sec:negative}), bumping a split-version field in the hash invalidated
the affected cells and left the random-split cells untouched, and the re-run
changed $0$ of the random rows. Trained checkpoints are content-addressed on
the same principle and, since the fix, on a digest of the realised train/val/test
partition rather than on the split's name, so a checkpoint cannot silently
outlive the partition it was trained for.

\paragraph{Device policy} Training uses the GPU; the attribution loop is
CPU-only, because the worker pool forks and a CUDA context does not survive
\texttt{fork}. This is a deliberate constraint, and it has a reproducibility
consequence worth stating: we verified that Integrated Gradients, Saliency,
Guided Backpropagation and Input×Gradient are bit-identical on GPU and CPU,
whereas GNNExplainer moves by $0.003$--$0.012$ because it fits a mask with Adam
over $\sim$100 steps. A reader reproducing on a GPU should expect to match
GNNExplainer to two decimals, not more. We also verified that the parallel
audit loop reproduces the serial one field-by-field over complete per-molecule
records, so cores may be traded for wall time freely.

One defect here was real and is worth recording. DIG's SubgraphX draws from
\texttt{np.random}, which \texttt{torch.manual\_seed} never resets, so its
output depended on how many molecules had been attributed before it --- results
that changed if a run resumed at a different point. Seeding per molecule from
(run seed, graph id) makes each attribution a function of the molecule alone,
which is both order-independent and resume-safe.

\paragraph{Cost} \ifHasCompute{}The sweep was run as a sequence of resumed
invocations, so the figures below are the final one --- \computeWall{} minutes
of wall clock, re-executing \nComputeFresh{} cell-runs and reusing
\nComputeCached{} from cache --- rather than a cold end-to-end total, which
this run never paid and which we therefore do not quote. What generalises is
the per-unit cost. Median training time for a (dataset, backbone, split, seed)
checkpoint is \trainMedian{}~s. Attribution is where the cost lives, and it is
not spread evenly: the median SubgraphX cell-run takes \cellSgxMin{}~minutes
against \cellIgMin{} for Integrated Gradients, a factor of
$\cellCostRatio{}$ at equal sample size.

We report that ratio as a finding rather than as housekeeping. Compute cost is
a selection pressure on which attributors get benchmarked at all, and it pushes
the literature toward cheap ones; a benchmark that silently caps the expensive
attributor's $n$ to fit a budget buys its coverage with exactly the small-sample
weakness the field criticises. Our own first instinct was to do that. We kept
SubgraphX at the full per-cell sample and reduced the number of cells instead,
which makes the coverage limit explicit (\S\ref{sec:limits}) rather than hiding
it inside a confidence interval.\fi{}
